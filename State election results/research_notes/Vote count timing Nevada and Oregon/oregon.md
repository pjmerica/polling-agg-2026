## 2. 2026 rules: vote by mail, return deadline, postmark rule, processing, first results, cure

### Takeaway
A ballot counts if either:
- it reaches a drop site or county office by 8:00 p.m. PST on Tue Nov 3 (11:00 p.m. EST; Mountain-time Malheur: 8:00 p.m. MST = 10:00 p.m. EST), or
- it is mailed with a postal indicator dated no later than Nov 3 and arrives by Tue Nov 10. A ballot with no or an illegible postmark counts as mailed on Election Day if it arrives by Nov 10.

The 7-day postmark rule is in force for 2026 (see section 5). Counties may open and scan ballots as soon as they arrive (ballots went out Oct. 14), but no tally may be made public until after 8 p.m. on Nov 3. The signature-cure deadline is Tue Nov 24.

### Cited Findings
**Return and postmark deadlines**
- ORS 254.470(1): drop sites "shall be open on the date of the election for a period of eight or more hours, but must be open until at least 8 p.m." — [ORS ch. 254, 2025 ed.](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)
- ORS 254.470(6)(e), mailed ballots — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html):
  - "(A) The ballot must be received at the office of the county clerk not later than the end of the period determined under subsection (1) of this section on the date of the election; or (B) The ballot must: (i) Have a postal indicator showing that the ballot was mailed not later than the date of the election; and (ii) Be received at the office of the county clerk not later than seven calendar days after the date of the election."
  - Computed 2026 date: Nov 3 + 7 = **Tuesday, Nov 10, 2026**.
- ORS 254.470(8), no postmark: "If the elector returns the ballot by mail, and a postal indicator is not present or legible, the ballot shall be considered to be mailed on the date of the election and may be counted if the ballot is received no later than seven calendar days after the election." — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)
- ORS 254.470(6)(f), wrong county: a ballot dropped in another county "shall be forwarded to the county clerk of the county in which the elector resides not later than the eighth day after the election." — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)
- The SOS 2026 calendar confirms the computed dates — [SOS 2026 Elections Calendar (primary)](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf):
  - Nov 3: Election Day. County offices open 7 a.m.-8 p.m.; drop sites open until 8 p.m. "Ballots that are mailed must be postmarked by election day. Ballots deposited in an official drop box must be received by 8 pm on election day."
  - Nov 10: "Last day to receive valid postmarked ballots by mail" (ORS 253.070; 254.470).
  - Nov 11: Veterans Day, no mail delivery.
  - Nov 12: "Last Day to forward ballots. Adjusted under ORS 187.010(3)."
- OAR 165-007-0045, "Counting Ballots Received by Mail" (effective Jan. 15, 2022; implements HB 3291 (2021)) — [OAR 165-007-0045 (primary)](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewSingleRule.action?ruleVrsnRsn=285827):
  - Ballots received by the 8 p.m. deadline are counted "without evaluating any postmark."
  - A ballot received later counts only if it came from USPS, arrived within seven calendar days, and was postmarked no later than Election Day.
  - "If a ballot return envelope contains more than one postmark, the earlier postmark is the official date of mailing."
  - For a missing or illegible postmark, officials may use USPS tools to fix the mailing date, otherwise a signed declaration on the envelope.
  - A late-arriving envelope with no signature is not counted.
  - Voters at an official drop site by 8 p.m. may finish voting.
- The AP's primary preview put it as "Mailed ballots must be postmarked by 8 p.m. local time," with drop-box deadlines of "10 p.m. ET for Mountain time areas and 11 p.m. ET for most of the state on Pacific time" — [AP Decision Notes, May 18, 2026](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/18/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-oregons-primaries/). The statute uses the date ("not later than the date of the election"), not 8 p.m.
- The SOS recommends mailing by Oct. 27 and "strongly encourages voters to return their ballot via a drop box" — [SOS Upcoming Elections](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/pages/current-election.aspx). SOS Tobias Read: "You can be confident your ballot will be postmarked and delivered on time if you mail it at least a week before election day" — [OPB/Oregon Capital Chronicle, Oct. 14, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/14/oregon-elections-chief-urges-early-voting-usps-service-slow-mail/).
- USPS says anyone can ask a post office to hand-stamp a ballot for free — [AP via PBS, Sept. 9, 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/ballots-discounted-for-late-postmarks-surged-in-washington-and-oregon-after-postal-service-change).

**Processing before Election Day and first results**
- ORS 254.478 lets counties start as soon as ballots arrive: "(1) Subject to ORS 260.705, upon receipt of ballots, the county clerk may: (a) Begin opening return identification envelopes of ballots and any used secrecy envelopes of ballots; and (b) In accordance with a security plan approved by the Secretary of State under ORS 254.074, begin scanning ballots into a vote tally system. (2) The county clerk may take any other actions that are necessary to count ballots." It sets no day count before the election — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html).
- ORS 254.074(4): a clerk "may not scan ballots as described in ORS 254.478 unless the Secretary of State reviews and approves a security plan" — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html). County security plans were due Jan. 31, 2026 — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).
- Ballot mailing: first day Oct. 14, 2026; last day to mail ballots to voters Oct. 20; replacement and absentee ballots by Oct. 29 — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).
- Ban on early results, ORS 260.705: "A person may not make public the results of the tally of votes from any precinct until after 8 p.m. on the date of the election." — [ORS ch. 260](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors260.html)
- OAR 165-007-0550 — [OAR 165-007-0550 via Cornell LII (mirror of official text)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-165-007-0550):
  - (9): counties must publish results to the state website "after 8 p.m. and before midnight, at least once" on Election Day.
  - (11): each Election Day public report must include the number of ballots accepted so far and unofficial results.
- Malheur County's 2024 schedule posted its first results at "9 p.m. (Mountain time), or 8 p.m. Pacific Standard Time" — [Oregon Capital Chronicle, Nov. 5, 2024](https://oregoncapitalchronicle.com/2024/11/05/state-and-most-counties-will-publish-first-unofficial-results-soon-after-8-p-m-in-oregon/).

**Signature cure**
- ORS 254.431(2)(a): "the elector must provide evidence sufficient to disprove the challenge not later than the 21st calendar day after the date of the election." ORS 254.431(3)(a): no identifying information on challenged voters may be released "until the 15th calendar day after the date of an election." — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)
- Computed 2026 dates: Nov 3 + 21 = **Tue Nov 24, 2026**; Nov 3 + 15 = Wed Nov 18. The SOS calendar lists Nov 18 (challenged-ballot information public) and Nov 24 ("Last day to resolve ballot challenges") — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).

### Inferences
- Under ORS 254.478, counties can have opened, signature-checked and scanned most pre-Election-Day ballots before 8 p.m. on Nov 3. That is why the first 8 p.m. PST (11 p.m. EST) release is large. Ballots dropped on Election Day still need signature checks, so they are counted Wednesday onward (section 4).
- Malheur's Mountain-time drop boxes close at 7 p.m. PST, but its 2024 practice was to release nothing until 8 p.m. PST. I infer the same in 2026 under ORS 260.705.
- In a close race, two late tails matter:
  - mail ballots postmarked by Nov 3 and arriving Nov 4-10;
  - signature cures through Nov 24, which counties report after the deadline (Multnomah on Nov 25; section 4).

### Gaps
- I found no source saying exactly which parts of Malheur County keep Pacific time.
- Whether "8 p.m." in ORS 260.705 means local time in Mountain-time areas is not addressed in the text.
- I found no 2026 SOS directive defining a "postal indicator" beyond OAR 165-007-0045.

## 3. Canvass, certification, recount (2026 calendar)

### Takeaway
- Counties must deliver abstracts by Mon Nov 30, 2026 (27th day). Multnomah, Washington and Clackamas all plan to certify by Nov 30.
- The Secretary of State canvasses U.S. Senate and House results by Thu Dec 10, 2026 (37th day).
- The governor's race is not canvassed by the SOS. Under Art. V §4 of the state constitution, the sealed returns go to the Speaker, who opens and publishes them before the Legislative Assembly.
- An automatic recount is triggered if the top-two margin is within 0.2% of their combined votes. There is no fixed recount timetable. Recount demands are due by Tue Dec 15, 2026.

### Cited Findings
**County canvass**
- ORS 254.545 — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html):
  - (1) "As soon as possible after any election, shall prepare abstracts of votes. The abstract for election of Governor shall be on a sheet separate from the abstracts for other offices and measures."
  - (3) "Not later than the 27th day after the election, shall deliver a copy of the abstracts for other than county offices to the appropriate elections officials. The abstract for election of Governor shall be delivered separately to the Secretary of State as provided in Article V, section 4, of the Oregon Constitution."
  - Computed: Nov 3 + 27 = **Monday, Nov 30, 2026**. The SOS calendar lists Nov 30 as "Last day to prepare and deliver abstracts" — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).
- County certification dates:
  - Multnomah: by Nov 30, 2026 — [Multnomah 2026 results schedule](https://multco.us/node/654374).
  - Washington: "Certified Final Results" Mon Nov 30, 4:00 p.m. — [Washington County](http://www.washingtoncountyor.gov/elections/current-election-results).
  - Clackamas: "no later than November 30, 2026" — [Clackamas County](https://www.clackamas.us/elections/november-3-2026-general-election-unofficial-results).

**SOS canvass and the governor**
- ORS 254.555(1): "not later than the 37th day after any election, the Secretary of State ... shall: (a) Canvass the votes for the offices, except the office of Governor after the general election ... (c) Prepare and deliver a certificate of nomination or election ... (d) Issue a proclamation declaring the election of candidates to the offices." — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)
- Computed: Nov 3 + 37 = **Thursday, Dec 10, 2026**. The SOS calendar lists Dec 10: "Last day for Secretary of State to canvass votes, prepare and deliver certificates of election and issue proclamation declaring election of candidates" — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).
- Measures are canvassed by the 30th day (Thu Dec 3) under ORS 254.555(2) — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html); [SOS calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf). No statewide measures are on the 2026 ballot — [SOS](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/pages/current-election.aspx).
- Oregon Constitution — [Oregon Constitution](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/Pages/OrConst.aspx):
  - Art. V §4: "the returns of every Election for Governor, shall be sealed up, and transmitted to the Secretary of State; directed to the Speaker of the House of Representatives, who shall open, and publish them in the presence of both houses of the Legislative Assembly."
  - Art. V §5: in a tie, "the two houses of the Legislative Assembly at the next regular session thereof, shall forthwith by joint vote, proceed to elect one of the said persons Governor."
  - Art. V §6: "Contested Elections for Governor shall be determined by the Legislative Assembly in such manner as may be prescribed by law."

**Post-election audit (hand count), 2026 dates**
- SOS names the contests and batches to hand count by Nov 25 (15 business days). Hand counts must begin by Nov 30 and finish by Dec 8. Amended candidate abstracts are due Dec 10 if a second hand count requires them — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf); ORS 254.529 in [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html).

**Recount**
- ORS 258.280(1): "The Secretary of State shall order a full recount of the votes cast for nomination or election to a public office for which the Secretary of State is the filing officer ... if the canvass of votes of the election reveals that: (a) Two or more candidates ... have an equal and the highest number of votes; or (b) The difference in the number of votes cast for a candidate apparently nominated or elected to the office and the votes cast for the closest apparently defeated opponent is not more than one-fifth of one percent of the total votes for both candidates." — [ORS ch. 258](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors258.html)
- No day count for recounts: ORS 258.221(1) says "The recount shall be completed as soon as practicable after the demand is filed." ORS 258.150(2)(a) lists Governor among offices for which the SOS "shall ensure that county elections officials conduct the recount." — [ORS ch. 258](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors258.html)
- Requested recounts: under ORS 258.161(8) the first demand is due "not later than the 42nd day" after the election — [ORS ch. 258](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors258.html). That is **Tue Dec 15, 2026**. The deadline to contest an election when there is no recount is Mon Dec 14 (40th day, adjusted) — [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf).
- The AP "may call a race that is subject to a recount if the lead is too large to be changed"; Oregon recounts are automatic for a tie or a margin of 0.2% or less — [AP Decision Notes, May 18, 2026](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/18/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-oregons-primaries/).
- 2024 analog: counties certified Dec 2, 2024 (the 27th day) — [Clackamas County Nov. 8, 2024](https://www.clackamas.us/news/2024-11-08/clackamas-county-elections-continues-to-accurately-and-efficiently-process-on-time-ballots-for-the-november-5-2024-general-election); [Washington County Nov. 5, 2024](https://www.washingtoncountyor.gov/home/news/2024/11/05/first-unofficial-election-results-november-2024-general-election-now-available).

### Inferences
- An automatic recount is triggered only once the canvass "reveals" the margin, so in practice it would follow the county abstracts (by Nov 30). That means a recount, if any, would run in December 2026.
- The governor's result is formally published by the Speaker before the 2027 Legislative Assembly, not certified by the SOS. For bettors, the practical resolution points are the AP call and the county abstracts (Nov 30).
- In 2022 the 0.2% band was about 3,535 votes. In 2026 it will be about 3,800-4,000. My calculation.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the date the 2027 Legislative Assembly convenes or how quickly the Speaker publishes governor returns in practice.
- I could not confirm whether the SOS's 37-day canvass deadline for candidates differed in 2022/2024. ORS 254.555 was amended by 2023 c.600 and 2024 c.56, but I did not read those session laws.

## 4. Reporting units, results sites, slow counties, reporting order

### Takeaway
**Election night.** Results go to the SOS site (results.oregonvotes.gov) and county sites starting just after 8:00 p.m. PST (11:00 p.m. EST). Each county must post at least once before midnight PST (3:00 a.m. EST). The first release is mostly ballots received through about Monday. Recent precedent: about 60-75% of the final vote by the end of election night.

**Election Day ballots.** Ballots received on Election Day were about 25% of all ballots in 2022 and 2024, and about 31-32% in Multnomah and Washington. They are counted Wednesday through Friday.

**Postmarked mail.** Mail arriving through Tuesday after the election adds about 0.6% of the vote (2024) to 1.7% (2022).

**Who updates when.** Multnomah posts daily at 6 p.m. PST Wednesday-Friday. Clackamas posts daily at 5 p.m. PST Wednesday-Friday. Washington County posts only Wednesday and Friday at 4 p.m. PST, then weekly on Fridays.

### Cited Findings
**Results sites and the reporting rule**
- The SOS and most counties publish first unofficial results "just after 8 p.m." on results.oregonvotes.gov, and most counties also post their own. Portland/Multnomah ranked-choice contests appear only on Multnomah's site — [Oregon Capital Chronicle, Nov. 5, 2024](https://oregoncapitalchronicle.com/2024/11/05/state-and-most-counties-will-publish-first-unofficial-results-soon-after-8-p-m-in-oregon/); [results.oregonvotes.gov](https://results.oregonvotes.gov).
- Multnomah publishes to the state Election Night Reporting system and its own site. "https://rcvresults.multco.us will be the sole source of results" for Multnomah and Portland candidate contests — [Multnomah 2026 schedule](https://multco.us/node/654374).
- OAR 165-007-0550, "Procedure for Reporting Elections Results" (adopted effective Feb. 17, 2022; amended effective Sept. 2, 2022) — [Cornell LII mirror](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-165-007-0550):
  - (3) For statewide elections, counties report directly to the SOS results site.
  - (5) Counties must publish a reporting schedule at least 30 days before the election.
  - (10) Results must be posted within one hour of public release.
  - (11) Each Election Day report must include the number of ballots accepted.
  - (12) After Election Day, counties report "as frequently as possible" per their schedule, weighing first the number of ballots remaining against registration and voter anonymity, then timeliness.
  - (13) Counties must track and report postmarked envelopes accepted and envelopes rejected as untimely.
  - The rule does not require publishing a count of ballots remaining.
- ORS 254.485(7) directs the SOS to set by rule how to announce "the status of the tally of the ballots received after the date of the election," prioritizing voter anonymity, then timely reporting — [ORS ch. 254](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html).

Hour-by-hour guide for Nov 3-6, 2026 (from the schedules and precedents above):

- **Tue 8:00 p.m. PST / 11:00 p.m. EST.** First state and county releases (Malheur at 9 p.m. MST, the same moment). This is mostly ballots logged through Monday, likely about 60-70% of the final vote, using 2022 (about 61% at 9 p.m. PST) and the 2024 primary (74% by end of night) as guides.
- **Tue 9:30 p.m.-midnight PST / 12:30-3:00 a.m. EST.** Second and third releases (Clackamas 9:30 and 11:30; Multnomah and Washington 10:00 and midnight). These add Monday ballots and Election Day morning drop-box pickups (the 2024 Lane pattern).
- **Wed 4:00-6:00 p.m. PST / 7:00-9:00 p.m. EST.** Washington 4:00, Clackamas 5:00, Multnomah 6:00. First big batches of Election Day drop-box ballots, after signature checks.

- **Nov 10-12.** Postmarked mail stops arriving Nov 10. Clackamas posts Nov 10 and Multnomah Nov 12. Expect about 0.5-2% of the total vote in this tail.
- **Nov 24-30.** Cures due Nov 24, reported Nov 25 (Clackamas, Multnomah). County certification Nov 30.

- Washington County's sparse schedule is the biggest blind spot. In 2024 about 31% of its ballots came on Election Day, and in 2026 they will appear only in the Wed and Fri 4 p.m. PST releases.

## 5. Law changes since 2022 that affect 2026

### Takeaway
None of the counting-timing rules changed between the 2022 and 2026 generals:
- the 7-day postmark grace period (HB 3291, 2021, first used in 2022);
- processing on receipt;
- the 21-day cure;
- the 27-day county canvass;
- the reporting rule (OAR 165-007-0550, 2022).

The 2025 bills that would have redefined "postal indicator" (SB 964) or ended vote-by-mail (SB 210) were not enacted, and the 2026 short session passed nothing on counting. Federal threats to the grace period failed in court:
- a permanent injunction for Oregon and Washington against the March 2025 executive order (Jan. 9, 2026);
- the Supreme Court's 5-4 ruling in Watson v. RNC (June 29, 2026) that federal Election Day statutes do not preempt post-Election Day receipt.

What did change in practice is USPS postmarking (December 2025 rule), which pushed late-postmark rejections up sharply.

### Cited Findings
**Pre-2022 baseline (in force in 2022, 2024 and 2026)**
- HB 3291 (2021) created the postmark grace period. Before it, ballots had to be received by Election Day — [KDRV/AP, Nov. 2022](https://www.kdrv.com/news/your-voice-your-vote-ap-explains-kotek-victory-declaration-while-drazan-waits-for-totals/article_a689e4f8-61ec-11ed-8388-8fae2b3fab46.html). **Federal actions**
- Executive Order 14248 (March 2025) sought proof of citizenship and receipt of all ballots by Election Day.
- On Friday, Jan. 9, 2026, U.S. District Judge John H. Chun (W.D. Wash.) "permanently blocked the Trump administration from enforcing" it as to Oregon and Washington, writing that "the president's authority to promulgate a national ballot-receipt deadline cannot stem from the Constitution."   - Holding: 3 U.S.C. §1 and 2 U.S.C. §§1, 7 do not bar counting ballots postmarked by Election Day and received afterward. "nothing in the federal-election day statutes requires ballots to be received by election day." The Fifth Circuit was reversed.
  - The 5-4 split is also reported by [NOTUS (search excerpt)](https://www.notus.org/courts/supreme-court-mail-in-ballots-election-day-watson).

### Inferences
- For 2026, counting runs on the same legal clock as 2022 and 2024. The only systematic differences are:
  - more postmark rejections, so somewhat fewer late mail ballots count;
  - SOS's push toward drop boxes, which may move more ballots to Election Day drop-offs (counted Wednesday-Friday) and fewer to the postmarked tail.
- With Watson decided and the Chun injunction permanent, a pre-election federal change to Oregon's grace period is very unlikely before Nov 3. Faegre Drinker separately noted that 2026 disputes would likely have to wait under the Purcell principle; that note came from a search excerpt of another law firm's summary and was not verified.

### Gaps
- I did not read the enacted texts of 2023 c.600 or 2024 c.56, so I cannot say exactly what changed in ORS 254.485 or 254.555 in 2023-24 (for example, whether the SOS candidate-canvass deadline was 30 days in 2022).
- I found no record of whether the Jan. 9, 2026 ruling was appealed.
- I did not find the HB 3474 USPS study report (due Sept. 15, 2026). The August 2026 USPS mail test reported by OPB may be part of it.

## 7. Partisan lean by return timing (2022, 2024) and by county

### Takeaway
Later returns in Oregon lean less Republican and more nonaffiliated.

By party registration of ballots received, the Democratic-minus-Republican gap statewide:
- **2024:** +8.5 points in ballots received through Monday morning; +11.0 for ballots received from Monday to Election Day evening; +14.3 for late Election Day drop-offs; +17.7 for post-Election Day mail.
- **2022:** +8.4 early, rising to about +12 for the final weekend and Election Day.

The main driver is geography. Multnomah and Washington take about 31-32% of their ballots on Election Day, against 16-25% elsewhere.

In vote terms, the Democrat's statewide margin grew after the first release:
- about +0.35 points (2024 president);
- about +1.1 points (2022 Senate, from 9 p.m. PST);
- about +2.4 points (2022 governor, from election night to final).

Within a single slow county the tail can lean Republican: Clackamas's late batch in OR-5 2022, and the final 2022 statewide tail after Friday.

### Inferences
- **The Wednesday-Friday count is the Democratic-leaning segment statewide.** It is dominated by Multnomah and Washington Election Day drop-offs and by nonaffiliated voters, who in Oregon lean Democratic, especially younger ones. A Kotek lead at the end of election night should usually grow by roughly 1-2.5 points. A Drazan lead of under about 2 points at midnight PST would be at real risk.
- **Watch for a partial reversal late in the week.** If Clackamas (or Marion and other Republican-leaning counties) still hold large Election Day batches on Thu-Fri, the last part of the count can shave a few tenths off a Democratic lead, as from 3.72 on Friday to 3.42 final in 2022.
- **The postmarked tail (Nov 4-10) leans less Republican** by registration (2024: R 16.6% vs. 28.2% overall). It is small, about 0.6-1.7% of ballots, and 2026's higher postmark rejection rate makes it smaller still. It matters only if the margin is under about 0.5 points.
- **Early 2026 returns are not predictive.** As of Oct. 21 they are 52% Democratic, against 39-40% Democratic for the early pre-Election-Day returns in 2022 and 2024. This early skew is typical, and the gap narrows sharply in the last four days.
