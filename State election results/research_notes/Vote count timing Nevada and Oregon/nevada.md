# Nevada: how the November 3, 2026 vote is counted and reported (focus: Governor, Lombardo (R) vs Ford (D)), and how close Nevada counts unfolded in 2018-2024

- Current as of 2026-10-22. Nov 3, 2026 is after daylight saving time ends (Sun Nov 1, 2026), so Nevada is on PST and the East on EST: **ET = PT + 3 hours**. The same offset applied on every historical election night cited below (Nov 6 2018, Nov 3 2020, Nov 8 2022, Nov 5 2024). Time conversions and day-of-week arithmetic are mine.
- Statute text is quoted from the Legislature's NRS chapter 293 page, which is stamped "[Rev. 9/9/2026]", so it reflects the 2025 session.

## 2. 2026 rules (poll close, "last voter in line," mail ballots, cure, provisionals, ID, processing, early-release ban)

### Takeaway
Polls close at **7:00 p.m. PST (10:00 p.m. EST) on Tue Nov 3**, but no results come out until everyone in line anywhere in the state has voted. Every active voter gets a mail ballot. Mail ballots count if:
- dropped off by poll close; or
- postmarked by Election Day and received by **5 p.m. PST Sat Nov 7** (8 p.m. EST); or
- carrying no readable postmark and received by **5 p.m. PST Fri Nov 6**.

Signature cures run to **5 p.m. PST Mon Nov 9** (8 p.m. EST). Provisional-ballot ID is due **5 p.m. PST Fri Nov 6**. Counting of mail ballots may begin **Mon Oct 19** and must finish by **Tue Nov 10**. Releasing any count before all voting ends is a crime. These rules are unchanged from 2022 and 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Poll hours.** Election Day vote centers are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., and "Voters in line by 7 p.m. may still vote." — [Washoe County 2026 General Election Timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php). AP's 2024 Nevada preview gives poll close as "10 p.m. ET." — [AP via PBS, "What to expect in Nevada on Election Day" (Oct 30, 2024)](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-nevada-on-election-day)
- **Voters in line.** NRS 293.305(1): if voters are "waiting in line to apply to vote" at closing, "the doors of the polling place must be closed after all those registered voters and electors have been admitted ... The registration of those electors and the voting by those registered voters and electors must continue until all such registration and voting has been completed." — [NRS 293.305](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec305)
- **Release ban, mail ballots.** NRS 293.269935(3): "No voting results of mail ballots may be released until all polling places are closed and all votes have been cast on the day of the election. Any person who disseminates to the public ... information pertaining to the count of mail ballots before all polling places are closed and all votes have been cast on the day of the election is guilty of a misdemeanor." — [NRS 293.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269935)
- **Release ban, early in-person votes.** NRS 293.3606(2): "The returns for early voting must not be reported until after the polls have closed on election day." Under (5), disseminating them early is a gross misdemeanor. Under (1), boards count early-voting returns in public "After 8 a.m. on election day." — [NRS 293.3606](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3606)
- **How this works in practice.** Clark says that in state and federal elections "tabulation starts AFTER the Secretary of State indicates all statewide voting has ended." Its 2026 page says results are "available after 7pm on Election Day after the Nevada Secretary of State has indicated all statewide voting has finished." — [Clark County, Tabulation](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/services/tabulation); [Clark County 2026 General Election Information Index](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/26g-info). AP: Nevada "does not release any vote results until the final voter in line has cast a ballot." — [AP via PBS (2024)](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-nevada-on-election-day)
- **Universal mail ballots.**
  - All active voters registered on or before Oct 20, 2026 are sent a mail ballot. The exceptions are voters who opted out by Sept 4, 2026 and inactive voters, who can still vote in person. — [Clark "Facts and Figures"](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:912d393b-be95-46a9-81f0-76326555ef45/original/as/facts-26g.pdf)
  - In-state ballots went out Oct 5-12, 2026 (window: "On or after the fifth Mon. before Election Day but not later than the fourth Mon."; NRS 293.269911(5)(a)(1) as amended by AB 148 (2025)). — [Clark County, Key Important Dates for the 2026 Elections (updated 11/26/2025)](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)
- **Receipt and postmark (NRS 293.269921(1)).** To count, a mail ballot must be either "(a) Before the time set for closing of the polls, delivered by hand to the county clerk, or any ballot drop box ...; or (b) Mailed to the county clerk, and: (1) Postmarked on or before the day of the election; and (2) Received by the clerk not later than 5 p.m. on the fourth day following the election." — [NRS 293.269921](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921)
  - Computed: 4th day after Tue Nov 3 is **Sat Nov 7, 2026, 5 p.m. PST (8 p.m. EST)**. Clark's calendar ("11/7/26 Sat ... fourth day after ED by 5pm"), Washoe's timeline and the SOS FAQ all say Nov 7. — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf); [Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php); [NV SOS FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/elections/election-resources/faqs) (snippet)
- **No or unreadable postmark (NRS 293.269921(2)).** "If a mail ballot is received by mail not later than 5 p.m. on the third day following the election and the date of the postmark cannot be determined, the mail ballot shall be deemed to have been postmarked on or before the day of the election." — [NRS 293.269921](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921)
  - Computed: **Fri Nov 6, 2026, 5 p.m. PST (8 p.m. EST)**, matching Clark ("11/6/26 Fri ... third day after Election Day by 5pm") and the Nevada Independent FAQ. — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf); [Nevada Independent 2026 FAQ](https://thenevadaindependent.com/elections/2026/faq)
- **Drop-off on Election Day.**
  - Hand delivery or drop box is allowed until poll close (NRS 293.269921(1)(a)). Each county must put "a ballot drop box at every polling place in the county, including ... early voting" (NRS 293.269921(3)). — [NRS 293.269921](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921)
  - Clark has 181 mail-ballot drop-off locations, 95 early-voting sites and 138 Election Day vote centers. — [Clark "Facts and Figures"](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:912d393b-be95-46a9-81f0-76326555ef45/original/as/facts-26g.pdf)
  - Washoe has 18 early-vote centers and 41 Election Day vote centers; drop boxes take ballots until 7 p.m. — [Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php)
- **Signature cure (NRS 293.269927(6)).** "the voter must provide a signature or a confirmation, as applicable, not later than 5 p.m. on the sixth day following the election." The clerk must contact the voter by mail, phone (if available) and electronic means "as soon as possible after receipt" (sub. 9). — [NRS 293.269927](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269927)
  - Computed: **Mon Nov 9, 2026, 5 p.m. PST (8 p.m. EST)**. The SOS cure page says "until 5 p.m. PT on November 9" and that "Assembly Bill 321 (NRS 293.269927) passed by the 2021 Legislature ... provides six (6) days following the election to cure." — [NV SOS Signature Cure page](https://www.nvsos.gov/elections/election-information/2026-election-information/signature-cure) (snippet)
  - ID owed with a mail ballot by first-time voters (NRS 293.269915(3)(b)(2)) is due on the same day. — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)
- **Provisional ballots (NRS 293.3085).** Clerks "shall not: (a) Include any provisional ballot in the unofficial results reported on election night; or (b) Open any envelope containing a provisional ballot before 8 a.m. on the Wednesday following election day." A ballot counts if the voter "provides the required identification ... not later than 5 p.m. on the Friday following election day." — [NRS 293.3085](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3085)
  - Computed: envelopes may be opened from **Wed Nov 4, 8 a.m. PST (11 a.m. EST)**; ID is due **Fri Nov 6, 5 p.m. PST**. Clark and Washoe both list Nov 6. — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf); [Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php)
- **Same-day registration produces provisional ballots.** Voters who register or update online or in person on or after Oct 21, 2026 may vote only in person, and "new registrants may only vote a provisional ballot." In-person same-day registration during early voting (Oct 17-30) is "Provisional Ballot Only." — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf). Same-day registrants must show a valid Nevada driver's license, state ID, DMV interim document or Tribal ID, plus proof of residency if the ID lacks the current address. — [Clark Fall 2026 Newsletter](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:228bc80b-da3b-42da-bc72-25cce75470d1/original/as/cced-newsletter-fall2026.pdf)
- **Voter ID.** Nevada has no general photo-ID requirement in 2026. ID is required only of certain first-time voters who registered by mail or computer without supplying ID; they must present photo ID or a utility bill, bank statement, paycheck or government document (NRS 293.2725(1)). — [NRS 293.2725](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec2725). The 2025 voter-ID bill (AB 499) was vetoed (see section 5). The constitutional amendment (Question 7) cannot take effect before 2028. — [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/how-different-will-nevadas-elections-look-in-2026)
- **When counting may start and must end (NRS 293.269931(1)).** "The mail ballot central counting board may begin counting the received mail ballots 15 days before the day of the election. The board must complete the count of all mail ballots on or before the seventh day following the election." — [NRS 293.269931](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269931)
  - Computed: counting may begin **Mon Oct 19, 2026** and must finish by **Tue Nov 10, 2026**.
  - Washoe began processing mail ballots at its Central Counting Place on Oct 9, livestreamed. — [Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php)
  - Clark posted a "Mail Ballot Processing Schedule, Oct. 17-20, 2026." — [Clark 2026 info index](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/26g-info)
- **Separate reporting by method.** Mail-ballot returns "must be reported separately from the other votes that were not cast by mail ballot in the precinct unless reporting the returns separately would violate the secrecy of a voter's ballot" (NRS 293.269935(1)). Early-voting returns are likewise reported separately (NRS 293.3606(3)). — [NRS 293.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269935); [NRS 293.3606](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3606)
- **Early voting** ran Sat Oct 17 to Fri Oct 30, 2026 (NRS 293.3568). — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)

### Inferences
- The first numbers cannot appear at 7:00 p.m. PST. They wait for the Secretary of State to declare voting over statewide, and lines anywhere, including rural counties, delay the release (see section 4 for 2024 times).
- Mail ballots dropped in boxes on Election Day still need signature verification and processing. They are therefore almost certainly not in election-night totals, consistent with 2022 and 2024 practice (section 4).
- Provisional ballots, which include all brand-new same-day registrants, cannot be in any count before Wednesday 8 a.m. PST. In practice they arrive late in the week or near the canvass.
- Under NRS 293.269931, the count of mail ballots, including cured ballots, should be essentially done by Tue Nov 10. After that, only provisionals and reconciliation remain before the Nov 13 canvass.

- I could not open the Secretary of State's own 2026 calendar or FAQ pages (Incapsula). Its dates are confirmed only through search snippets and the matching Clark and Washoe calendars.
- No 2026 statement gives the expected number of same-day-registration provisional ballots.

## 3. Canvass, certification, recount (2026 calendar)

### Takeaway
- County canvasses are due **Fri Nov 13, 2026**.
- The Nevada Supreme Court and the Secretary of State canvass statewide races on **Tue Nov 24, 2026**.
- A defeated statewide candidate (governor) must demand a recount **within 3 working days after the Supreme Court canvass**, by **Tue Dec 1, 2026, 5 p.m.**, and must prepay the estimated cost.
- For House races the deadline is 3 working days after the county canvass: **Wed Nov 18, 2026**.

A recount must start within 5 days of the demand and finish within 5 days of starting. Nevada does not use the AP call or a "certified by county" step to declare a winner; results stay unofficial until the canvass.

### Cited Findings
- **County canvass.** NRS 293.387(1): "The canvass must be completed on or before the 10th day following the election." The abstract goes to the SOS "on or before the 10th day following the election" (293.387(3)); see also NRS 293.393(1). — [NRS 293.387](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec387); [NRS 293.393](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec393)
  - Computed: 10th day after Nov 3 is **Fri Nov 13, 2026**. Clark lists "11/13/26 Fri Canvass Deadline." Washoe's Board of County Commissioners meets Nov 13 at 10 a.m. PST to canvass. — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf); [Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php)
- **Statewide canvass.** NRS 293.395(2): "On the fourth Tuesday of November after each general election, the justices of the Supreme Court, or a majority thereof, shall meet with the Secretary of State, and shall open and canvass the vote for ... United States Senator, Representative in Congress, members of the Legislature, state officers who are elected statewide ..." Under (3), the Governor then issues certificates of election. — [NRS 293.395](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec395)
  - Computed: Tuesdays in Nov 2026 fall on the 3rd, 10th, 17th and 24th, so the 4th Tuesday is **Tue Nov 24, 2026**. Clark's calendar lists "11/24/26 Tue Deadline for Nevada Supreme Court's Canvass." — [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)
- **Recount demand.** NRS 293.403(1): a defeated candidate "may demand and receive a recount ... if, within 3 working days after the canvass of the vote and the certification ... of the abstract of votes, the candidate ... (a) Files in writing a demand ... and (b) Deposits in advance the estimated costs of the recount." Under 293.403(4)(c), for a general election "canvass" means "(1) The canvass by the Supreme Court of the returns for a candidate for a statewide office ...; or (2) The canvass of the board of county commissioners ... for any other candidate." — [NRS 293.403](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec403)
  - **Statewide (Governor):** Clark lists "12/1/26 Tue Recount Request Deadline (Statewide) ... Within 3 working days after the Nevada Supreme Court's canvass, by 5pm."
  - **County and multi-county (House):** Clark lists "11/18/26 Wed Recount Request Deadline."
  - Source: [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)
- **Recount timing and cost.** NRS 293.405(3): "each recount must be commenced within 5 days after demand, and must be completed within 5 days after it is begun." Under 293.405(1)-(2), a requester who loses pays any shortfall; a requester who wins is refunded, and for statewide offices the state repays counties. — [NRS 293.405](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec405)
- **Election contests.** NRS 293.413(1): a statement of contest is due "no later than 5 days after a recount is completed, and no later than 14 days after the election if no recount is demanded." Clark lists the no-recount deadline as Tue Nov 17, 2026. — [NRS 293.413](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec413); [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf). NRS 293.407(2) removes "the general election for the office of Governor, Lieutenant Governor, [legislators], justice of the Supreme Court" from the district-court contest procedure. — [NRS 293.407](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec407)
- **Risk-limiting audits** are required (NRS 293.394). — [NRS chapter 293](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html)
- **Precedent:** in 2024, Clark found 1,608 timely mail ballots that had never been tabulated (they were stuck in duplication) during reconciliation before its Nov 15 canvass. It added them and certified, and officials said no outcome changed. — [Fox5 Vegas, Nov 15, 2024](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2024/11/15/1608-mail-ballots-found-by-clark-county-ahead-certification/)

### Inferences
- Working-day count for the statewide deadline: Wed Nov 25 (1); Thu Nov 26 (Thanksgiving) and Fri Nov 27 (Nevada's "Family Day") are holidays; Mon Nov 30 (2); Tue Dec 1 (3). This matches Clark's Dec 1. Treating Family Day as a holiday is my assumption, but it agrees with the official calendar.
- Even a recount demanded on Dec 1 would, by statute, finish by about Dec 11 (5 days to start plus 5 days to complete).
- Because governor contests are excluded from the district-court route, a contested governor result would go to a different forum (presumably the Legislature). I did not verify the alternative procedure.
- I found no automatic-recount provision in the sections reviewed; a recount must be demanded and paid for.

### Gaps
- I did not quote the statute governing contests of a Governor general election, or any recount cost estimates.

## 4. Reporting units, results sites, slow jurisdictions, reporting order, ballots left after election night

### Takeaway
Statewide results post on the Secretary of State's site, with Clark and Washoe posting their own pages. Clark casts about **69-70% of the statewide vote** and Washoe roughly a fifth (21% of early 2026 ballots). The first release comes only after the last voter in line statewide has voted:
- **2020:** 8:41 p.m. PST.
- **2024:** voting ran past 9:30 p.m. PST in Washoe and Nye.

The first release holds early in-person votes plus mail processed before Election Day. Election Day in-person votes follow overnight. Mail ballots dropped off on Election Day and mail arriving through Saturday are counted from Wednesday on:
- **Washoe 2026:** posts **by noon and 5 p.m. PST (3 p.m./8 p.m. EST)** each day.
- **Clark 2024:** posted Wednesday afternoon and evening, then **by 8 p.m. PST (11 p.m. EST) Thu-Sat**. Clark has not published a 2026 schedule.
- **Ballots left after election night:** more than 100,000 statewide in 2022, about 100,000 in Clark plus Washoe in 2024.

### Inferences
Expected 2026 sequence, built from the cited patterns; times are PST / EST:
- **7:00 / 10:00 p.m.:** polls close. Nothing posts while anyone is in line statewide.
- **About 9:00-10:30 p.m. / midnight-1:30 a.m.:** first release, likely the bulk of the final vote. It contains early in-person votes (Oct 17-30) and mail processed before Election Day. In 2020 the first votes came at 8:41 p.m. PST; in 2024 the last voters were still voting at 9:30-9:45 p.m. PST.
- **Late night to about 2 a.m. / 5 a.m.:** Election Day in-person votes from vote centers, including Clark's overnight update (1:32 a.m. PST in 2024). Clark's new 2026 paper-ballot tabulators at vote centers might change this timing; that is untested in a general election (section 5).
- **Wed Nov 4 to Sat Nov 7:**
  - Clark's large batch of Election Day drop-offs (about 54,000-57,000 in 2022 and 2024) is expected Wednesday afternoon or evening, then Clark daily drops in the evening (6-8 p.m. PST / 9-11 p.m. EST in 2022-2024).
  - Washoe posts at noon and 5 p.m. PST.
  - Mail arriving by Saturday 5 p.m. PST keeps feeding these batches.
- **Mon Nov 9 to Thu Nov 12:**
  - Cures close Monday at 5 p.m. PST, so cured ballots (7,000-12,000 in Clark in 2022 and 2024) land Tue-Thu. The mail count must be finished by Tue Nov 10.
  - Provisionals, including same-day new registrants, are added before the Fri Nov 13 canvass.
  - Veterans Day (Wed Nov 11) falls in this window and may slow county work. This is my assumption.
- **Size of the post-election-night pool:** about 100,000 ballots or more (2022: more than 144,000 in Clark plus Washoe roughly 18 hours after polls closed; 2024: about 100,000 in Clark plus Washoe on Wednesday). In a race decided by 1-2 points, that is enough to reverse an election-night lead (section 6).

## 5. Law changes since 2022 that affect 2026

### Takeaway
Almost nothing about counting changed for 2026.
- **2025 session:** Lombardo vetoed the main election bills (AB 499 voter ID, AB 306 drop boxes, AB 534 omnibus, AB 597). The enacted changes (AB 148, AB 491) do not touch receipt, postmark, cure or reporting rules.
- **Courts:** the no-postmark rule survived the Nevada Supreme Court (Oct 28, 2024). The U.S. Supreme Court's *Watson v. RNC* (June 29, 2026) held that federal Election Day statutes do **not** preempt post-Election Day receipt of timely-cast mail ballots, so Nevada's 4-day window stands for federal races too.
- **Process:** the one practical change is Clark's new paper-ballot in-person process (ballot-marking device, then print, then scan).
- **Earlier rule:** the 7-day receipt window used in 2020 had already been cut to 4 days in 2021, before the 2022 election.

### Cited Findings
- **Baseline: AB 321 (2021).** NRS 293.269921 (4-day receipt, 3-day no-postmark rule, drop boxes), 293.269927 (6-day cure), 293.269931 (counting window) and 293.269935 (release ban) were all "Added to NRS by 2021." — [NRS 293.269921-.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921). The SOS credits AB 321 (2021) for the 6-day cure. — [NV SOS Signature Cure](https://www.nvsos.gov/elections/election-information/2026-election-information/signature-cure) (snippet)
- **Rule that applied in 2020 but has since changed.** In 2020, "Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day could be counted until Tuesday" (Nov 10, 2020, seven days after the election). — [AP explainer via News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/explainer-why-ap-called-nevada-and-the-whole-presidential-race-for-biden). The 2022, 2024 and 2026 rule is four days (NRS 293.269921).
- **AB 499 (2025), vetoed June 12, 2025.**
  - It would have required in-person voters to show photo ID and mail voters to give the last four digits of an ID number.
  - Lombardo's veto message: "Because AB 499 would apply voter ID requirements unequally between in-person and mail ballot voters and fails to sufficiently guarantee ballot security, I cannot support it."
  - Sources: [Ballotpedia News](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/06/16/nevada-gov-lombardo-vetoes-bills-to-require-voter-id-and-open-primaries-to-unaffiliated-voters/); [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/lombardo-vetoes-voter-id-bill-pitched-as-compromise-with-legislative-democrats) (snippets)
  - Reporting on the deal said Lombardo had wanted mail ballots to be due by the close of Election Day, but Democrats refused. — [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/lombardo-vetoes-voter-id-bill-pitched-as-compromise-with-legislative-democrats) (snippet)
- **Other 2025 vetoes and failures:**
  - AB 597 (nonpartisan primary participation): vetoed.
  - AB 534 (omnibus including a grant program for local election offices): vetoed because it "centralizes too much authority" in the SOS.
  - AB 306 (more drop boxes before Election Day): vetoed for insufficient security.
  - AB 79 (campaign finance): vetoed.
  - SB 74 (candidate filing, training money): died in the session's final minutes.
  - "Republican efforts to impose stricter mail ballot deadlines and signature-curing rules did not become law."
  - Source: [Nevada Independent, "How different will Nevada's elections look in 2026?"](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/how-different-will-nevadas-elections-look-in-2026). A search summary of the same article says the Secretary of State's top priority, faster counting, "largely fell flat" (snippet).
- **Enacted 2025 changes (none affect counting):**
  - **AB 148 (2025):** a new in-state mail-ballot distribution window, "On or after the fifth Mon. before Election Day but not later than the fourth Mon." (Oct 5-12, 2026; NRS 293.269911(5)(a)(1)), and new sample-ballot deadlines (NRS 293.565(6)).
  - **AB 491 (2025):** candidate residency and registration (NRS 293.1755).
  - Source: [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)
  - The Nevada Independent also lists more than $30 million for the SOS's centralized voter-registration and election-management system. — [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/how-different-will-nevadas-elections-look-in-2026)
- **No-postmark ballots, Nevada Supreme Court (Oct 28, 2024).**
  - The court upheld counting mail ballots with no or unreadable postmark received up to 3 days after Election Day. That rejected the Trump campaign, Nevada GOP and RNC, whose request a Carson City judge had denied in August 2024.
  - The result was unanimous, but Justices Herndon and Pickering concurred on other grounds. Herndon wrote that the law "is clear and unambiguous that a mail ballot must contain a postmark" but found no irreparable harm.
  - The GOP had identified 24 such ballots received after Election Day in the June 2024 primary.
  - Sources: [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/nevada-supreme-court-rules-non-postmarked-ballots-can-be-counted-within-3-days-of-election); [KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/political/elections-local/nevada-supreme-court-rejects-rncs-argument-that-certain-mail-in-ballots-shouldnt-be-counted)

- ***Watson v. Republican National Committee*, No. 24-1260 (U.S., decided June 29, 2026), 5-4.**
  - The Court reversed the Fifth Circuit: the federal Election Day statutes govern when votes are cast, not when ballots must be received. Federal Election Day statutes "do not preempt state receipt deadlines for timely cast mail ballots."
  - Justice Alito dissented, joined by Thomas and Gorsuch. Sources disagree on whether Kavanaugh joined fully (The Arc) or in part (Wikipedia).
  - Sources: [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/supreme-court-holds-federal-election-day-statutes-do-not-preempt-state-receipt-deadlines-for-timely-cast-mail-ballots/); [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/24-1260/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee); [The Arc](https://thearc.org/blog/supreme-court-protects-mail-voting-in-major-win-for-voters-with-disabilities/)

  - Officials say it is meant to reduce errors and speed counting. A county official named adjudication of unclear marks as the main past bottleneck. — [KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/big-change-coming-for-nevada-voting-in-2026-election-cycle) (snippet)

- **Not in effect for 2026:** an interim legislative committee (Aug 2026) directed staff to draft a bill requiring Clark and Washoe to record a returned mail ballot within 12 hours of arrival and review signatures within 24 hours. - **New SOS regulations:** I found none that change counting or reporting for 2026 (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- For bettors, the 2026 rules match 2022 and 2024 (4-day receipt, 3-day no-postmark, 6-day cure, 10-day canvass, Supreme Court canvass on the 4th Tuesday). The 2022 and 2024 batch patterns are therefore the right template.
- *Watson* removes the main federal legal risk to counting ballots that arrive Nov 4-7. A last-minute suit seeking to exclude them from the NV-1 to NV-4 House races is now very unlikely to succeed. The governor's race was always governed by state law.
- The Herndon concurrence leaves the no-postmark rule open to future litigation, but the number of such ballots is tiny (24 in the 2024 primary).
- Clark's paper-ballot tabulators could speed the Election Day in-person count, or slow it if they cause any problems. Either way, this process has not yet been tested in a high-turnout general election.

- A search summary mentioned SJR 1, a special-session joint resolution to protect vote-by-mail in the constitution; I could not verify its source or status. It cannot affect 2026 counting in any case.
- The specific 2023 amendments to the recount, contest and risk-limiting-audit sections (NRS 293.394, .403, .405, .413 show "2023" in their histories) were not reviewed.

## 6. History, 2018-2024: who led, how leads moved, calls, final margins

### Takeaway
- **Pre-universal-mail 2018:** races were called on election night.
- **Universal-mail era:** close statewide races took **3-4 days to call**: Sat Nov 7, 2020; Fri Nov 11, 2022 (Governor); Sat Nov 12, 2022 (Senate); late Fri Nov 8, 2024 PST. Post-Election Day batches moved the margin toward Democrats each time.
- **NV-Sen 2022:** Laxalt led by about 19,000-23,000 the morning after; Cortez Masto overtook him on the 4th day (Saturday) and won by 7,928.
- **NV-Gov 2022:** Lombardo led throughout and won by 15,386.
- **NV-Sen 2024:** Brown led narrowly on election night; Rosen passed him Wednesday night and won by about 24,000.
- **NV-Pres 2024:** Trump led throughout and won by 46,008.

## 7. Partisan lean by vote method and by counting time (2022, 2024; Clark vs Washoe vs rurals)

### Takeaway
Three patterns held in both cycles:
- Early in-person voting leans Republican.
- Mail leans Democratic.
- Votes counted after Election Day have been "heavily Democratic" (AP).

**2022:**
- By party registration of the ballots cast: Republicans won early in-person by about 50,000 ballots and Election Day by roughly 2-to-1; Democrats won mail by about 70,000.
- By candidate vote, late Clark batches ran about 61-63% Cortez Masto.

**2024:** Republicans' in-person edge grew to about 97,000 ballots, and Election Day was roughly even by party. Democrats won mail by about 66,000 (9 pts), but the late mail surge helped Democrats less than expected.

**Geography:** Clark leans Democratic (by about 27,000 votes for Harris in 2024), Washoe is the swing county, and the rurals are heavily Republican (+31 in 2026 early ballots).

### Cited Findings
- **Party registration of ballots by method.** These are counts of ballots by the voter's registered party, not candidate votes; source is Jon Ralston's tallies. — [Nevada Independent, early voting blog 2026 general](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-early-voting-blog-2026-general)
  - **2022:** "In-person was 28 percent of turnout, and Republicans won it by about 50,000 ballots (49% to 31%). Mail was 51 percent, and Democrats won it by about 70,000 ballots (14 points). Election Day was about a fifth of the vote, and Republicans won it by roughly 2-to-1 and almost 50,000 ballots." Clark mail went 2-to-1 Democratic.
  - **2024:** "In-person was 36 percent of the vote, and Republicans won it by about 97,000 ballots (18 points). Mail was 47 percent, and Democrats won it by about 66,000 ballots (9 points). Election Day was under 17 percent, with Republicans and Democrats essentially tied."
  - Ralston expected "a late mail surge to help Democrats catch up" in 2024 and later wrote that this was wrong.
- **AP on late votes:** "votes counted after Election Day have been heavily Democratic" in recent elections. ### Inferences
Likely lean of each 2026 batch, extrapolated from 2022 and 2024:
- **First release** (early in-person plus pre-Election Day mail): mixed. Early in-person leans R and pre-Election Day mail leans D. With mail at 63% of ballots so far and Democrats +4 by registration, the first drop could be close in the governor's race. Non-major-party voters (about a quarter of ballots) decide the candidate split, which party-registration data cannot show.
- **Election Day in-person** (overnight): leaned strongly R in 2022 and was even by party in 2024. Expect it to lean Lombardo.
- **Election Day drop-offs and late-arriving mail** (Wednesday to the following Tuesday): leaned strongly D in 2020 and 2022 (57-63% D in Clark batches) and more weakly in 2024. Expect these to lean Ford, by an amount between those two cycles. This is the main uncertainty for anyone reading an election-night Lombardo lead.
- **Cures** (Tue-Thu after Nov 9) and **provisionals/same-day registrants** (by Nov 13): small volumes; direction not documented in my sources.
- **Rurals** report quickly and are R +30 or more. Any rural ballots still outstanding late in the week favor Lombardo, but they are too few to matter much (AP judged them unable to close Clark and Washoe gaps in 2022 and 2024).

## 8. What the Secretary of State, Clark and Washoe have said about 2026 timing; AP's 2026 "What to expect"

### Takeaway
Officials have given **process and deadline guidance, not forecasts**:
- Results post on Results.NV.gov after polls close and stay unofficial until the Nov 13 canvass.
- Clark posts after the SOS declares voting finished statewide.
- Washoe posts at noon and 5 p.m. PST daily after Election Day, warns that late mail and postal changes could delay results, and noted that 20% of primary mail arrived on election night.
