## 2. 2026 rules: poll close, early processing, result release, ballot deadlines, cure, canvass, recount

### Takeaway
- **Poll close:** 7:00 p.m. local. That is 8:00 p.m. ET for most of the state and 9:00 p.m. ET for El Paso and Hudspeth.
- **Early counting:** Counties of 100k+ may begin *counting* early ballots when early voting ends (Oct 30). They may not announce any results until polls close.
- **Unofficial results:** must be released "as soon as available" after polls close. Election Day totals are due within 24 hours.
- **Mail ballots:** due 7 p.m. Election Day, or postmarked by then and received by 5 p.m. Nov 4.
- **Military/overseas ballots and cures:** both have a Nov 9 deadline.
- **Last day for counting:** Nov 16.
- **Local canvass:** Nov 6-17.
- **State canvass:** no earlier than Nov 21.

### Cited Findings (primary source: SOS Advisory 2026-21, *November 3, 2026 Election Law Calendar*)
All of the following come from the [SOS Election Law Calendar, Advisory 2026-21](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml):

- **Early voting** runs Oct 19 - Oct 30, 2026. Oct 30 is the last day of in-person early voting (Sec. 85.001(a)). The mail-ballot application deadline was Oct 23, received-by (Sec. 84.007).
- **Early counting by county size:**
  - **100,000+ population** counties using electronic systems: the early voting ballot board / central count *may begin counting at the end of the early voting period* (Sec. 87.0241(b)(2)).
  - **Under 100,000:** the board may *process* mail ballots but may not *count* until polls open on Election Day (Secs. 87.0221, 87.023, 87.024, 87.0241).
- **Results embargo:** Results "may not be announced until after polls close," even where counting begins early (Secs. 87.0222, 87.0241).
- **Election Day hours:** polls are open 7:00 a.m. - 7:00 p.m. (Sec. 41.031).
- **Release on election night:**
  - The central counting station must release unofficial results "as soon as available after polls close," but may withhold totals until the last voter has voted (Sec. 127.1311).
  - Presiding judges may announce unofficial precinct totals after polls close (Sec. 65.015).
- **Domestic mail ballot deadline:**
  - Ballots must be received by 7:00 p.m. on Election Day (Sec. 86.007(a)(1)).
  - **Or** postmarked (cancellation or carrier receipt mark) by 7:00 p.m. Election Day **and received by 5:00 p.m. Wed Nov 4, 2026** (Secs. 86.007, 101.057).
  - Ballots without a mark are not counted if they arrive late.
- **Military/overseas deadline:** overseas ABBM voters and military/FPCA voters have until **Mon Nov 9, 2026** (Secs. 86.007(d), (d-1), (e), 101.057, 101.001).
- **Mail-ballot defect cure** (e.g., ID number mismatch, the SB 1 corrective-action process): the voter must cure in person at the early voting clerk's office by **Nov 9, 2026** (Secs. 87.0271, 87.0411).
- **Provisional ballots:**
  - Ballots go to the voter registrar by Nov 4 (Secs. 65.052, 65.053).
  - Voters have until **Nov 9** to present ID, a reasonable-impediment declaration or an exemption (Secs. 65.054, 65.0541).
  - The registrar must finish its review by Nov 13 (Sec. 65.052).
- **Last day for counting: Mon Nov 16, 2026.** This is the last day the early voting ballot board can convene to count:
  - late domestic ballots;
  - late overseas and military ballots;
  - reviewed provisional ballots;
  - cured carrier envelopes (Secs. 86.007, 87.125(a), 65.051(a), 86.011(d)).
  - Any meeting after that date needs a court order.
- **Local canvass:**
  - Earliest is **Nov 6**, and only after all provisional and timely mail and overseas ballots are counted (Sec. 67.003).
  - Last day is **Nov 17** per the SOS calendar (Sec. 67.003).
  - Caveat: the WebFetch summary flagged that the statute text says "not later than the 11th day after election day" (= Nov 14), which conflicts with Nov 17. Unresolved; the SOS calendar may reflect a business-day or holiday rule.
- **State canvass:** the Governor may first conduct it **Nov 21, 2026** (Sec. 67.012).
- **Audits:**
  - Statewide risk-limiting audit on Nov 19 (Sec. 127.302).
  - Post-election hand-count audit to finish by Dec 3 (SB 827 (2025), Sec. 127.201).
- **2025 law changes noted in the calendar:**
  - **SB 2217:** per-storage-device ballot count reports; reports before polls open may not show votes (Sec. 127.1302).
  - **SB 2166:** central count tabulators must be tested three times.
  - **HB 1522:** canvass notice is now 3 business days.
  - **HB 521:** curbside voting attestation.
  - **Video surveillance:** required in 100k+ counties (Sec. 127.1232).

### Other sources on these rules
- **24-hour rule:** state law requires Election Day totals to be submitted to the state within 24 hours after polls close. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process); [Votebeat, 2026-03-09](https://www.votebeat.org/2026/03/09/2026-texas-primary-election-problems-wrong-polling-places-hand-count-delay-results/)
- **Continuous counting:** once counting begins it cannot stop until finished, and large counties work in shifts. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process)
- **Turnout reconciliation:** counties must post a comparison of voters checked in vs. ballots cast. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process); the reconciliation report is under Sec. 127.131(f) per [SOS news release 2022-11-07](https://www.sos.texas.gov/about/newsreleases/2022/110722.shtml)
- **Pre-Election Day mail counting:** counties over 100,000 may begin counting received mail ballots after polls close on the last day of early voting. Smaller counties start the morning of Election Day. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process)
- **Two time zones:** polls closed at 8 p.m. and 9 p.m. ET depending on the part of the state (2024). — [AP via Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/politics/2024/11/06/why-ap-called-the-texas-senate-race-for-ted-cruz/)

### Inferences
- Large counties can pre-tabulate early in-person and mail ballots from Oct 30 on. Their first post-7 p.m. drop should therefore contain nearly all early votes. That holds for Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, Fort Bend, El Paso and Hidalgo, all 100k+.
- Nothing is allowed out before polls close statewide? **Not confirmed.** The embargo is tied to "polls close". In practice, CT counties post at about 7 p.m. CT, before El Paso and Hudspeth close at 8 p.m. CT; the 2024 AP explainer notes the staggered 8/9 p.m. ET closes. Whether the SOS portal holds statewide display until 8 p.m. CT was not verified.
- **Post-election-night changes:** after election night, the only additions are:
  - late postmarked domestic mail (through Nov 4, 5 p.m.);
  - military/overseas ballots (through Nov 9);
  - cured mail ballots and provisionals (through Nov 9-16).

  Mail was about 3.3% of the 2024 vote (see section 4). These late categories are therefore small and could move the margin by only a few tenths of a point at most. That matters only if the race is within about 0.5 pt.

### Gaps
- **"Every 90 minutes" posting requirement:** no Texas statute or source was found. The searches turned up nothing; the SOS portal itself refreshes every 5 minutes. Treat this as unverified or likely another state's rule.
- **Exact statute for the 24-hour reporting rule:** reported by the Tribune and Votebeat. The section number was not confirmed; Sec. 127.1311 governs "as soon as available."
- **Recount rules:** not researched to primary-source level. These include the petition threshold (believed to be a margin below 10% of the winner's vote for a candidate-requested recount) and the deadlines (Elec. Code Title 13, ch. 212). Verify before relying on them.
- **SB 2753 (2025):** shifts the 12-day early-voting window to run up to Election Day. It would delay the early-vote drop.
  - The 2026 SOS calendar still shows early voting Oct 19-30, ending the Friday before Election Day. So SB 2753 appears **not** in effect for Nov 2026 (inference from the calendar).
  - Its effective date was not confirmed; SOS must report to the Legislature on readiness by Aug 2027. — [Votebeat 2025 roundup](https://www.votebeat.org/texas/2025/06/20/legislative-roundup-election-law-early-voting-by-mail-proof-of-citizenship/)

## 3. Reporting mechanics, order and cadence; slow counties

### Takeaway
There are 254 counties. Each county enters totals into the SOS "TEAM" system, and SOS posts them unedited at results.texas-election.com, refreshed every 5 minutes. Order:

1. Early in-person plus mail, shortly after 7 p.m.
2. Election Day vote-center and precinct ballots, trickling in overnight.

The big urban counties (Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Collin) are the slowest on Election Day ballots.

### Cited Findings
- **SOS portal:**
  - Address: https://results.texas-election.com/races
  - Updated "every 5 minutes" until all precincts report.
  - "Results from Early Voting are submitted by Texas counties and reported first."
  - SOS does not alter county data.
  - Counties' own sites may update before the SOS portal.
  - The official canvass typically occurs "10-14 days after Election Day."
  - Source: [SOS release 2022-11-07](https://www.sos.texas.gov/about/newsreleases/2022/110722.shtml)
- **Data entry and review:** election workers manually enter race totals into the statewide TEAM system, and SOS reviews county reports and checks discrepancies. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process)
- **Ballot transport:** Election Day ballots and equipment are driven to central count. Large counties use substations: Harris has 6 and Tarrant has 7. In Collin, transport can take up to an hour after polls close. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-10-31](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process)
- **Size of the first drop:** in Harris County 2020, 88% of the county's votes were in the first batch at 7 p.m. Results in Harris, Dallas, Tarrant and Collin were expected to be "particularly slow" for Election Day ballots. — [ABC13, 2024](https://abc13.com/post/officials-coordinate-counting-ballots-coming-hundreds-voting-locations-texas-ahead-2024-election-day/15493731/)
- **Harris County:**
  - Problems on Election Day 2022: incorrect mapping, wrong ballot styles, not enough paper, paper jams.
  - In 2023, **SB 1750** abolished the Harris County elections administrator post. Duties returned to the county clerk and tax assessor-collector, and the Texas Supreme Court let it take effect Sept 2023.
  - **SB 1933** (2023) lets SOS intervene in Harris County on "good cause to believe" there is a recurring pattern of problems.
  - Sources: [Election Law Blog](https://electionlawblog.org/2023/houston-area-elections-office-dismantled-as-contentious-texas-law-takes-effect/); [Texas Tribune 2023-05-23](https://www.texastribune.org/2023/05/23/texas-legislature-harris-county-elections); [Spectrum News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/tx/south-texas-el-paso/politics/2023/08/24/court-allows-new-law-to-eliminate-harris-county-election-chief-); [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/bellaire-meyerland-west-university/city-county/texas-house-approves-bills-that-grant-state-oversight-over-harris-county-elections/)
- **Harris 2022 primary:** Harris failed to meet the state 24-hour deadline. Its then-elections chief (Longoria) told lawmakers the combination of rules was "untenable" for large elections. — [Texas Tribune via search summary](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/10/31/texas-2024-election-results-counting-process); [Houston Chronicle via PressReader, 2022-05-12](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/houston-chronicle/20220512/281573769289250)
- **March 3, 2026 primary: a dress rehearsal with delays.** — [Votebeat 2026-03-09](https://www.votebeat.org/2026/03/09/2026-texas-primary-election-problems-wrong-polling-places-hand-count-delay-results/); [Courier Texas](https://couriertexas.com/dfw/2026/03/05/wtf-happened-during-the-dallas-county-primary-election-night)
  - **Dallas County:** results were significantly delayed. The county GOP's switch to precinct-based voting (required for its hand count) forced Democrats to switch too. Voters were turned away at former vote centers. The Texas Supreme Court stayed a judge's order extending hours to 9 p.m. Democrats had 2,316 provisional ballots.
  - **Williamson County:** extended hours were also stayed; 541 provisionals across both primaries.
  - **Hand-count counties:**
    - Calhoun missed the 24-hour deadline and finished Friday.
    - Eastland reported about 5:45 p.m. Wednesday.
    - Gillespie reported after 5 a.m. Wednesday.
- **Travis County (2026 primary):** early ballots were not counted in advance, so initial results might not be available right at poll close. — [KUT, 2026-03-03](https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-03-03/travis-county-tx-austin-election-results-2026-texas-primary) (via search summary; not opened)
- **Tarrant County:** in July 2026, commissioners were weighing a cut in Election Day sites from 316 to 176. The final outcome was not confirmed. — [Hoodline, 2026-07](https://hoodline.com/2026/07/tarrant-county-weighs-slashing-140-election-day-polling-sites/)
- **Who runs the count:** primaries are run by county parties, usually outsourced to the county. The general is run by the county election authority. — [Votebeat 2026-03-09](https://www.votebeat.org/2026/03/09/2026-texas-primary-election-problems-wrong-polling-places-hand-count-delay-results/)

### Inferences
- **Party hand counts:** the March 2026 delays came mostly from party-run primary choices. County parties do not run the November general, so these specific causes should not carry over. Treat this as an inference.
- **Pace on Nov 3:** expect about 75-80% of the statewide vote at roughly 7:00-8:30 p.m. CT, as early-vote drops arrive.
- **Election Day pace:** smaller and rural counties' Election Day precincts tend to finish within a few hours. Harris, Dallas and Tarrant Election Day ballots run past midnight.
- **Possible Harris delay:** SB 1933 oversight could add process steps, but no source reports this.

- No source found on how Harris performed on election night in 2024.
- No source found on statewide percent reported by midnight CT in 2018-2024.

## 4. Vote mode shares and partisan lean

### Takeaway
In 2024, about 76% of the vote was early in-person, 3% mail and 20% Election Day. Texas mail voting is limited: age 65+, disability, absence from county, jail or confinement.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 vote by mode (EAVS):**

  | Mode | Ballots | Share |
  |---|---|---|
  | Early in-person | 8,703,181 | 75.8% |
  | Mail | 384,221 | 3.3% |
  | Election Day in-person | 2,329,171 | 20.3% |
  | Other | — | 0.6% |

  Turnout was 57.0%. — [EAC 2024 EAVS Data Brief: Texas](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/2024_EAVS_Data_Brief_TX.pdf)
- **2020 vs. 2024 early voting:** 9.7M voted early or by mail in 2020 (57.2% of registered voters). The 2020 early period was 3 weeks because of COVID. The 2024 figure was 9M+ (48.6%). Mail ballots fell from 937,870 (2020) to 347,652 (2024, preliminary). — [Texas Tribune, 2024-11-04](https://api.texastribune.org/2024/11/04/texas-early-voting-turnout-rate-down/)

### Inferences
- **Size of late swings:** the Election Day share (~20%) is the main source of post-7:30 p.m. movement. Mail (~3%) is skewed toward voters 65+ by eligibility rules. In Texas that typically leaned Republican in recent cycles, but that is **unverified**: no source was found.
- **Direction of the shift:** no reliable 2024 or 2022 statewide breakdown was found of D vs. R share by mode. Nationally the post-2020 pattern is that Election Day votes lean R. The 2018 Texas early drop showed O'Rourke ahead before Cruz overtook him (see section 5), consistent with a "red shift" through the night. Treat the magnitude as unknown.

### Gaps
- Partisan lean of early in-person vs. Election Day vs. mail in Texas, 2018-2024. No sourced figures were found. Precinct-level files from the SOS or Harris/Dallas county canvass reports would be needed.
- 2018 and 2022 mode shares were not retrieved.

## 5. Historical: call times and night-of dynamics

### Takeaway
Every recent Texas Senate race was called on election night: 2018 at about 9:30 p.m. CT, 2024 at 10:39 p.m. CT. In 2018 the first (early) returns had O'Rourke ahead before Cruz overtook him.

### Cited Findings
- **2018 Senate (Cruz +2.6, about 219k votes):**
  - The first early-vote results gave O'Rourke a lead, and the lead traded back and forth for about 3 hours.
  - Fox called the race at about 9:25 p.m. CT. — [Texas Tribune 2018-11-06](https://www.texastribune.org/2018/11/06/ted-cruz-beto-orourke-texas-midterm-election-results)
  - AP call time conflicts between sources: 9:35 p.m. CT per [The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2018/11/07/texas-is-changing-ted-cruz-clings-to-us-senate-seat-after-competitive-challenge-from/); 9:27 p.m. CT per [Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/?p=1081027) (search-snippet level; not opened).
- **2020 President:**
  - AP projected Trump the Texas winner at 12:13 a.m. Wednesday (Trump 52.2%). — [SMU Daily Campus](https://smudailycampus.com/?p=1057700) (search-snippet level)
  - The time zone was not stated. Likely CT, but unverified.
- **2020 Senate (Cornyn 52.4 - Hegar 45.2):**
  - AP had not called the race when Hegar trailed by about 7 points with more than 45% counted. — [AP via Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2020/nov/3/hegar-concedes-senate-race-to-cornyn-trump-doctor-/)
  - Decision Desk HQ reportedly called it at about 8:31 p.m. CT; snippet figures were garbled, so treat as unreliable.
  - Hegar conceded on election night.
  - The exact AP call time was not found.
- **2022 Governor (Abbott vs. O'Rourke):** AP called it election night; Abbott "never trailed." The basis was Abbott's wide lead in the 13 counties he won in 2018. — [AP via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/why-ap-called-texas-governor-041929806.html). The exact call time was not stated.
- **2024 Senate (Cruz vs. Allred):** AP called it at **11:39 p.m. ET (10:39 p.m. CT)** with about 76% of the vote counted "from almost every county," and Cruz leading by 10+ points. — [AP via Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/politics/2024/11/06/why-ap-called-the-texas-senate-race-for-ted-cruz/)
- **2026 GOP runoff:** AP called it about an hour after polls closed (Paxton +25). — [ABC7/AP](https://abc7ny.com/post/texas-attorney-general-ken-paxton-wins-gop-runoff-us-senate-ousting-longtime-sen-john-cornyn-ap-projects/19177494/)

### Inferences
- **Where counting stands at calls:** 76% counted at 10:39 p.m. CT in 2024 roughly equals the early-vote share. So most Election Day votes were still outstanding about 3.5 hours after poll close.
- **What a call needs in 2026:** with a race inside about 3 pts, AP would need much of the Election Day vote in Harris, Dallas, Tarrant and Bexar. That points to a call after midnight CT or on Nov 4, if the margin is under about 1-2 pts.
- **Early-lead direction:** the 2018 pattern (D leads the early drop, R gains on Election Day ballots) is the base case for an early Talarico lead that narrows. The size of the shift is unquantified (see section 4 gaps).

### Gaps
- AP call times for 2020 Senate and 2022 Governor.
- Percent counted by midnight CT for each year.
- Size of the early-to-final shift (early-drop margin vs. final margin) for each race. AP and SOS archives or NYT timestamped results would be needed.

## 6. What Texas SOS has said about 2026 result timing

### Takeaway
No 2026-specific SOS statement on result timing was found. The standing SOS guidance (2022 release) is:
- early vote reported first;
- 5-minute portal refresh;
- unofficial results until canvass;
- canvass typically 10-14 days after Election Day.

### Cited Findings
- SOS 2022 election night guidance (above). — [SOS 2022-11-07](https://www.sos.texas.gov/about/newsreleases/2022/110722.shtml)
- SOS 2026 calendar and 2026 releases: the Oct 5 registration deadline release and Advisory 2026-21 contain no timing projection. — [SOS 2026-09-28 release](https://www.sos.texas.gov/about/newsreleases/2026/092826.shtml); [Advisory 2026-21](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml)

### Gaps
- An SOS pre-election press release for Nov 2026 (typically issued the week before) was not yet found or published as of 2026-10-21.
