# Alaska: vote counting, reporting order and timing for the Nov 3, 2026 general (U.S. Senate: Sullivan (R, inc.) vs. Peltola (D))

Researched 2026-10-21. Rules come from the Alaska Statutes (akleg.gov) and Division of Elections (DOE) pages. Historical vote-type splits were computed from DOE precinct result files: `results/24GENR/ENRbyPrecinct.csv` and `results/22GENR/Results_per_Precinct_2022_11_30T14_17_52_To Excel.txt`. No vote-type file was found for 2020 or 2018 (the 20GENR path returned 404).

## 1. 2026 rules: RCV status, poll closing, deadlines, count dates, RCV tabulation, certification, recounts

### Takeaway
RCV applies on Nov 3, 2026. The repeal (2026 Ballot Measure 2) is on the same ballot and cannot affect this election's count. Polls close at 8pm Alaska time, which is midnight ET.

Election night reports only:
- votes cast at polling places on Election Day;
- early votes cast Oct 19–29.

Absentee ballots, late early votes and questioned ballots are counted on scheduled days after the election: Nov 10 (day 7), Nov 13 (day 10) and Nov 18 (day 15). Nov 18 is also the receipt deadline for overseas ballots. RCV tabulation, if needed, runs on Nov 18. Certification is targeted for Nov 25.

**RCV and the repeal measure**
- 2026 Ballot Measure 2 is certified for the Nov 3, 2026 ballot. 42,837 signatures were verified across 40 House districts. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Alaska_Repeal_Top-Four_Ranked-Choice_Voting_Initiative_(2026)); [ADN 2025-12-31](https://www.adn.com/politics/2025/12/31/another-initiative-to-repeal-open-primaries-and-ranked-choice-voting-set-to-appear-on-alaskas-2026-ballot/)
- Measure 2 would:
  - repeal the 2020 system (open top-4 primaries and RCV generals);
  - restore closed party primaries and plurality generals;
  - remove the 2020 disclosure rules.
  — [Alaska Public Media 2026-10-05](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)
- The 2024 repeal measure failed 49.9% Yes to 50.1% No. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Alaska_Repeal_Top-Four_Ranked-Choice_Voting_Initiative_(2026))
- Alaskans will elect the U.S. Senator with RCV in 2026. The top four primary finishers advanced. — [Alaska Public Media 2026-10-05](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)
- The 2020 measure that created RCV did not change the statutory counting timeline, so the schedule would not change if Measure 2 passes. — summarised by search from [Election Law Blog 2026](https://electionlawblog.org/2026/simple-measures-to-speed-up-the-counting-of-rcv-ballots-in-alaska/) and [ADN op-ed 2026-09-27](https://www.adn.com/opinions/2026/09/27/opinion-want-faster-election-results-modernize-alaskas-vote-count/). Both pieces are by former Alaska election officials, who argue the 15-day wait is practice rather than a requirement of RCV. I did not read either piece in full.

**Hours and first results**
- Polling places are open 7am–8pm on Nov 3. Unofficial results are posted on the DOE website after 9pm. — [DOE 2026 General Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.4
- DOE expects the first unofficial results between 8:45pm and 9pm. More updates follow, but their number and timing vary. DOE posts each update on Facebook and X, emails media contacts, and says when it is done for the night. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.5

**What is counted on election night**
- Election night includes ballots cast at polling places on Election Day and ballots cast at Early Voting locations from Oct 19 through Oct 29. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.5
- Early votes cast through the Thursday before Election Day are counted on election night. Early votes cast from the Friday before Election Day through Election Day are counted seven days after the election. — [DOE Counting System & Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- Absentee ballots:
  - The Absentee Review Board begins reviewing ballots for eligibility by Oct 27.
  - "Some Absentee By-Mail and Absentee In-Person ballots will be counted on Election Day with subsequent counts taking place up to the 15th day."
  - Regional offices "begin counting absentee ballots on Election Night for those that have been received and processed up through a certain date."

  — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.5; [DOE Counting Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- By statute, review of absentee voter certificates starts no later than 7 days before the election. Counting of reviewed absentees "shall begin at 8:00 p.m., local time, on the day of the election." The district board certifies its absentee review no later than the 15th day. — [AS 15.20.201](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.201)
- Questioned ballots:
  - The Questioned Board begins its review on Nov 5. — [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)
  - Counting begins 7 days after Election Day and must finish by day 15. — [DOE Counting Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- DOE statement on absentee and questioned ballots: "All absentee ballots must be counted no later than … 15 days after the general election." The same deadline applies to questioned ballots. — [DOE Counting Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)

**Ballot receipt rules**
- A ballot returned by mail must be mailed by Election Day and received by close of business on the 10th day after the election. If postmarked, it must be postmarked on or before Election Day. "After the day of the election, ballots may not be accepted unless received by mail." — [AS 15.20.081(e)](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.081)
- Ballots mailed from outside the U.S., or by qualifying overseas voters, must be received by close of business on the 15th day after a general election. — [AS 15.20.081(h)(2)](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.081)
- There are no drop boxes. Absentee ballots can be handed in at DOE offices or any voting location until 8pm on Election Day. Return postage is not prepaid. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), pp.6
- Absentee ballots are not mailed to every voter; voters must apply. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.6

**Cure process and signatures**
- "State law does not provide for a cure process. Once a ballot is received by the division, no changes can be made by the voter." — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.6
- DOE does not verify signatures on absentee ballots. A witness or attesting official is required. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.6; [AS 15.20.081(d)](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.081)

**RCV tabulation and reports**
- On election night DOE publishes an Election Summary Report and a Statement of Votes Cast. Both show first-choice results only. The "RCV Detailed Report" is published "once tabulation is completed on November 18." — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.10
- Later RCV rounds are run by the Director's Office "following the last counting of ballots on the 15th day." — [DOE Counting Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- How the tabulation works:
  - Tabulation continues until two candidates remain, unless someone has a first-round majority.
  - Write-ins are resolved only if they collectively finish first, or second within 0.5%.
  - A ballot becomes inactive after two consecutive skipped rankings.

  — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), pp.5, 16

**Results site and reporting units**
- Results are posted at www.elections.alaska.gov/election-results/ in PDF, .txt and .xml. The txt/xml files may lag the PDFs. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), pp.9–10
- There are 271 ImageCast scanner precincts. Their results are transmitted to the Director's Office. — [DOE Counting System](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- There are 131 hand-count precincts, mostly rural. Their results are phoned in to regional offices and then submitted to the Director's Office. — [DOE Counting System](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- Early, absentee, questioned and electronic-transmission ballots are centrally counted by House district at the five regional offices. — [DOE Counting System](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)
- In results files these appear as "District N – Absentee / Early Voting / Question" rows, plus an "HD99 Fed Overseas Absentee" row. — DOE 2024 ENRbyPrecinct.csv (computed)

**Recounts**
- A defeated candidate or 10 voters may apply for a recount within 5 days after the state review is completed (3 days for Governor/Lt. Governor). — [AS 15.20.430](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.430)
- No deposit is required, and the state pays, if the margin is 20 votes or fewer or under 0.5% of the two candidates' combined votes. Otherwise the deposit is $15,000 statewide. — [AS 15.20.450](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.450)
- Recounts are automatic only in a tie. — [Alaska Public Media 2024-11-16 (correction note)](https://alaskapublic.org/2024/11/16/election-update-begich-declares-victory-margin-for-ranked-choice-repeal-now-under-900-votes/)

**Swearing-in**
- The U.S. Senator is sworn in Jan 3, 2027. — [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)

### Inferences
- **Time conversion.** DST ends Sunday Nov 1, 2026, so on Nov 3 Alaska is on AKST (UTC−9), the western Aleutians on HST (UTC−10) and the East Coast on EST (UTC−5).
  - Polls closing at 8pm AKST = 12:00am ET, Wed Nov 4.
  - Polls closing at 8pm HST in the Aleutians west of 169°30′W = 1:00am ET.
  - DOE's first results (8:45–9pm AKST) = about 12:45–1:00am ET Nov 4. Election-night reporting usually runs to about 2–4am ET (inferred; no official end time).
- **No formal call before Nov 18 if no one reaches 50% of first choices.** In a close Peltola–Sullivan race where minor candidates take a few percent, the winner is not known formally until the Nov 18 RCV report. A media call could come earlier only if:
  - one candidate is mathematically over 50% of first choices with too few ballots left, or
  - the first-choice leader's margin is big enough that transfers cannot overturn it. DDHQ did this in 2024, calling the House race on Nov 16.
- **The repeal measure has no effect on this count.** If 2026 Measure 2 passes, it changes future elections; the Nov 3, 2026 count is still run under RCV.
- **There is a gap between the two DOE documents.** The media packet's FAQ lists only Election Day and early (Oct 19–29) ballots for election night. The statute and the counting page say reviewed absentees may begin counting at 8pm. In practice, any absentees counted on election night have been a small share (see section 3). Treat the bulk of absentees as counted Nov 10–18.

## 2. Law changes since 2022 that affect the 2026 count

### Takeaway
None affect counting timing. The main bill, SB 64 (ballot tracking, curing and prepaid postage), was vetoed. The legislature failed to override it on May 4, 2026, so 2026 runs under the same rules as 2022 and 2024, with no cure process.

### Cited Findings
- SB 64 would have created:
  - ballot tracking;
  - a ballot-curing process for absentee ballots;
  - paid return postage;
  - a shorter timeline for removing inactive voters.
  — [Alaska Public Media 2026-04-30](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/alaska-legislature/2026-04-30/gov-dunleavy-vetoes-election-reform-bill-citing-concerns-over-timeline-ahead-of-2026-election)
- Gov. Dunleavy vetoed SB 64 around Apr 30, 2026. He said the tracking and curing system could not be responsibly built before the November election. — [Alaska Public Media 2026-04-30](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/alaska-legislature/2026-04-30/gov-dunleavy-vetoes-election-reform-bill-citing-concerns-over-timeline-ahead-of-2026-election)
- The override failed 38–22 on May 4, 2026. It needed 40 votes. — [Alaska Public Media 2026-05-04](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/alaska-legislature/2026-05-04/veto-override-vote-on-elections-bill-fails-as-two-southeast-alaska-lawmakers-flip); [Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/05/04/legislature-fails-override-bipartisan-elections-bill-veto/)
- On May 7 the Governor introduced a new elections bill. The House Speaker said there was no time left in the session to take it up. — [ADN 2026-05-07](https://www.adn.com/politics/alaska-legislature/2026/05/07/gov-dunleavy-introduces-new-elections-bill-days-after-vetoing-last-version/)
- DOE's 2026 media packet confirms there is still no cure process and no signature verification. — [DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf), p.6
- 2026 Ballot Measure 3 would reword the citizenship clause of the voting-eligibility statute. It does not touch counting. — [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)

### Inferences
- No procedural change since 2024 should alter the shape or timing of the count. The 2022 and 2024 patterns (section 3) are the best template.

## 3. Historical patterns 2018–2024: election-night share, margin shifts, call dates, partisan lean by vote type

### Takeaway
Absentee ballots, roughly 21–23% of the total, lean clearly Democratic or anti-Trump-Republican compared with Election Day votes. Election-day votes lean Republican. As a result, the Republican or more conservative side leads on election night and loses ground as absentees are counted on days 7–15. The size of the swing:

| Year and race | Swing as absentees were counted |
|---|---|
| 2022 Senate | Tshibaka's election-night lead of about 1.7 pts became a Murkowski win of 0.8 pts on first choices |
| 2024 Measure 2 | "Yes" led on election night and lost by about 700 votes |
| 2020 Senate | Sullivan's margin of about 57.9k before absentees shrank to about 45.0k |

Recent AP or media calls of close races came only on the RCV day (Nov 23, 2022; Nov 20, 2024), except DDHQ's earlier 2024 call.

### Inferences
- **Direction of the shift.** In every recent cycle the post-election-night count moved toward the Democrat or the less-conservative option:

  | Race | Post-election-night ballots vs. Election Day |
  |---|---|
  | 2024 House | Absentees about 11 pts more Peltola (two-party) than Election Day |
  | 2022 Senate | Absentees +16 Murkowski vs. Election Day −6 |
  | 2024 Measure 2 | Absentees about 7.5 pts more "No" |

  Early votes (partly counted on election night) also lean less R than Election Day in 2022 races. In 2024 they were about equal for the House race.
- **Implication for 2026.** In a true toss-up that ends roughly 50/50, Sullivan should lead the election-night report. A rough estimate from the 2024 House pattern: Election Day plus early votes counted on election night are about 70–75% of the final, and that subset ran about 2–3 pts more R in margin than the final. On that basis, Peltola would need to trail by no more than about 2–4 pts on election night to be on track. Questioned and late-early ballots (counted from day 7) leaned R in 2024, which partly offsets the absentee gain.
- **The path is not monotonic.** In 2024 Begich's lead grew from +5.8k (11pm partial) to +10.1k before the Nov 12 count, as late-reporting precincts and late early votes came in. It then fell to +6.7k as absentees were counted.
- **Election-night share.** In 2024 Election Day ballots were about 52% of the total. Adding most early votes, roughly 65–75% was likely reported on election night; this is inferred, because the CSV does not split early votes into election-night and day-7 batches. In 2022 precinct votes were 62.5%, so election night was about 70–75%.
- **Call timing.** A close Alaska race should not be expected to be called before Nov 10–16. If it goes to RCV and is within a few points, expect the call on Nov 18, the RCV report day. The 2022 Senate and House calls came on RCV day, Nov 23. The 2024 House was called by DDHQ on Nov 16 and became final on RCV day, Nov 20.
- **How RCV transfers might fall.** The 2026 transfers depend on who the 3rd and 4th candidates are. In 2022, Chesbro's (D) transfers went mostly to Murkowski over Tshibaka. In 2024, Howe's (AIP) transfers took Begich from 48.4% to 51.3%.
