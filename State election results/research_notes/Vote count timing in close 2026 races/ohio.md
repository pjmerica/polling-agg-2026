# Ohio: vote counting, reporting order and timing for the Nov 3, 2026 general (Gov: Acton v. Ramaswamy; U.S. Senate special: Brown v. Husted)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Primary-source rules were read directly on codes.ohio.gov. ohiosos.gov press releases returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so figures from them come from search snippets and syndicated copies (Highland County Press, Toledo Legal News), and are labeled that way.

## 1. 2026 rules: poll close, absentee receipt deadline, processing, provisionals, canvass, recount

### Takeaway
Polls close at 7:30 pm ET. **For Nov 2026 there is no post-Election-Day grace period for domestic absentee ballots.** SB 293 (136th GA, signed by DeWine Dec 2025, in effect from March 20, 2026) requires receipt by close of polls; only military and overseas (UOCAVA) ballots get extra time. Boards may pre-process and pre-scan absentees but may not tabulate or release them before polls close. Provisional voters have 4 days to cure (Sat Nov 7, 2026). County canvasses must be finished by day 21 (Tue Nov 24, 2026). The automatic recount threshold is 0.25% statewide.

### Cited Findings
- **Absentee receipt deadline.** ORC 3509.05(D), current version in effect March 20, 2026 under SB 293 of the 136th GA, requires return envelopes to reach the board "not later than the close of the polls on the day of an election". The section contains no postmark rule. Its exceptions cross-reference ORC 3511.11 (the UOCAVA section). — [ORC 3509.05](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3509.05)
- **SB 293 signing and scope.**
  - Signed into law by Gov. DeWine in December 2025.
  - Requires absentee ballots to arrive by the close of polls, with "no grace period for mail-in ballots".
  - Military and overseas voters are exempt.
  - Sources: [Van Wert Independent (search snippet)](https://thevwindependent.com/news/?p=206719); [Statehouse News Bureau, 2025-11-19](https://www.statenews.org/2025-11-19/ohio-lawmakers-vote-to-eliminate-the-four-day-grace-period-for-mail-in-ballots); [13abc, 2025-11-20](https://www.13abc.com/2025/11/20/ohio-moves-eliminate-absentee-ballot-grace-period/)
- **Earlier grace periods.** Ohio allowed 10 days through 2022 (ballots postmarked by the day before the election). HB 458 (2023) cut this to 4 days for 2024. — [Statehouse News Bureau](https://www.statenews.org/2025-11-19/ohio-lawmakers-vote-to-eliminate-the-four-day-grace-period-for-mail-in-ballots); [Van Wert Independent snippet](https://thevwindependent.com/news/?p=206719)
- **The 2024 rule in practice.** For 2024 the final count included late absentee ballots postmarked by Nov 4 that arrived after Election Day. — [Highland County Press, LaRose certification release, 2024-12-03](https://www.highlandcountypress.com/headlines/larose-certifies-official-2024-general-election-results)
- **Other SB 293 provisions.**
  - The bill also changes voter-roll maintenance and provisional voting, and the membership of the Ohio Election Integrity Commission.
  - It passed both chambers on party lines.
  - DeWine had said he saw no need for more voting restrictions.
  - Source: [Statehouse News Bureau](https://www.statenews.org/2025-11-19/ohio-lawmakers-vote-to-eliminate-the-four-day-grace-period-for-mail-in-ballots)
- **Federal pressure and the Supreme Court.**
  - DeWine signed "reluctantly", citing a pending U.S. Supreme Court case from Mississippi and DOJ warnings that it might sue Ohio.
  - The Court later upheld Mississippi's grace period 5-4, which by extension validates other states' grace periods.
  - Because Ohio had already acted, Ohio voters must still get ballots in by Election Day "this November".
  - Sources: [Ohio Capital Journal / States Newsroom headline + search summary, ~2026-06-30](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/?p=39988); [WYSO 2025-12-23](https://www.wyso.org/2025-12-23/civvoting-rights-group-disappointed-dewine-signed-law-cutting-ohios-absentee-ballots). The full article could not be fetched (403).
- **Effect of the new deadline in the 2026 primary.** Late-arriving absentees fell from 7,042 in the 2024 primary to 1,564 in the May 2026 primary, after a statewide education campaign. — [Ohio SoS 2026-06-03 release, via search snippet](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2026-06-03)
- **Pre-processing.** ORC 3509.06(F), in effect April 7, 2023 (HB 458):
  - The board "shall process absent voter's ballots before the time for counting those ballots", including sorting and scanning them on tabulating equipment if it can scan without tabulating.
  - The board "shall not tabulate or count the votes on those ballots before that time".
  - Division (G) bars disclosing "any portion of the count ... prior to the time of the closing of the polling places".
  - The statute sets no start date for processing.
  - Source: [ORC 3509.06](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3509.06)
- **Drop boxes.**
  - At most one secure receptacle, outside the board's office, on board property.
  - Open from the day after registration closes until 7:30 pm on Election Day, under 24/7 recorded video.
  - Emptied by a bipartisan team at least daily and at 7:30 pm on Election Day.
  - Source: [ORC 3509.05(C)(3)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3509.05)
- **Provisional cure.** Voters must appear at the board "within four days after the day of the election" to supply photo ID or other documentation. This applies to ballots cast without ID, religious-objection affidavits and challenged voters. Current version in effect March 20, 2026 (SB 293). — [ORC 3505.181](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3505.181)
- **County canvass.** The board must begin "not earlier than the fifth day or later than the fifteenth day" after a general election and "complete the canvass not later than the twenty-first day after the day of the election". Version in effect Oct 3, 2023 (HB 33). — [ORC 3505.32](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3505.32)
- **2024 certification timeline (precedent).**
  - Boards ran an "expedited canvass" and sent final counts by Nov 20, 2024.
  - The SoS release said LaRose had 10 days to certify. Day 10 fell on a Saturday, so he certified Monday, Dec 2, 2024.
  - Sources: [Ohio SoS 2024-11-20 release (search snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2024-11-20); [Ohio SoS 2024-12-02 (snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2024-12-02)
- **Automatic recount.** Margins under 0.25% of the total vote trigger an automatic recount in statewide races, and under 0.5% in district races. AP may still call a recount-eligible race. — [AP "What to expect in Ohio" (2024), via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day)
  - The SoS 2024 certification release mentions a 0.5% trigger, in the context of OH-9, a district race. — [Highland County Press](https://www.highlandcountypress.com/headlines/larose-certifies-official-2024-general-election-results)
- **2026 calendar.**
  - UOCAVA ballots mailed from Sept 18; all other absentee ballots from Oct 6.
  - Registration deadline Oct 5.
  - Early in-person voting began Oct 6.
  - Sources: [Ohio SoS week in review 2026-09-04 (snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/week-in-review/week-in-review-2026-09-04); [Spectrum News 2026-10-06](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/10/06/early-voting-kicks-off-in-ohio)

### Inferences
- **Key dates for Nov 3, 2026:**
  - Provisional cure deadline: Sat Nov 7.
  - County canvass window: opens Nov 8–18, must close by Tue Nov 24.
  - SoS certification: likely late Nov or early Dec, following the 2024 pattern.
- **What is left after election night.** With no domestic grace period, only UOCAVA ballots (small) and provisionals remain to be added. The election-night count should be a much more complete share of the final than in 2022 (10-day window) or 2024 (4-day window).

### Gaps
- The exact UOCAVA post-Election-Day window under ORC 3511.11 after SB 293 is unverified. One snippet says UOCAVA voters "still get four extra days"; that is consistent with the old rule, but I did not read the statute.
- The fetch tool could not read SB 293's full text, any litigation against it, or the exact Supreme Court case name and date. No lawsuit against SB 293's deadline turned up in searches, but none being found is not proof that none exists.
- The statute that sets the 10-day SoS certification period was not located; the figure comes from the SoS's own release.
- Pre-processing start dates in SoS directives are unverified.

## 2. Reporting units, official results site, cadence and order

### Takeaway
Ohio has 88 county boards. The first statewide dump, shortly after 7:30 pm ET, is the pre-tabulated absentee by mail plus early in-person vote, which leans Democratic. Election Day precinct votes follow over roughly the next 2–4 hours. Historically the race is effectively counted by midnight.

### Cited Findings
- **First wave.**
  - The first votes reported after polls close are mail and early in-person votes, which "have tended to favor Democrats".
  - In 2020 the first votes were reported at 7:39 pm ET.
  - About 95% of total votes were in by midnight ET in 2020.
  - Source: [AP What to Expect 2024 via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day)
- **Early-vote embargo.** Results from early and absentee voting are not released until polls close, and they are the first results reported. — [search summary of Columbus Dispatch / AOL 2024 article](https://www.aol.com/nearly-1-3-million-voted-183732288.html) (page now 404; summarized from snippet only)
- **Pre-election share of the vote.** About 59% in 2020 and about 35% in 2022. — [AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day)
- **Outstanding-ballot reporting.** The SoS website shows the number of outstanding absentee ballots, and "the numbers will change between election night and the final certification". — [Business Journal Daily, 2024](https://businessjournaldaily.com/?p=97042) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Results are presumably posted at the SoS Election Night Reporting site (ohiosos.gov live results); I did not verify the exact 2026 URL.
- **Reading the first dump.** Expect it at ~7:35–8:00 pm ET and expect it to show Brown and Acton with inflated leads. It is a share-of-vote artifact, not a signal.

### Gaps
- I found no county-level history of which counties are slowest. Large urban counties (Cuyahoga, Franklin, Hamilton) are commonly cited anecdotally, but I did not verify this.
- The exact results-site URL and refresh cadence for 2026 are unverified.

## 3. Law changes since 2022 affecting 2026

### Takeaway
Three changes since 2022 matter for 2026:
- **HB 458 (2023):** strict photo ID, one drop box per county at the board office, grace period cut 10→4 days, provisional cure 7→4 days.
- **SB 293 (2025, effective March 20, 2026):** grace period eliminated for non-UOCAVA ballots, plus provisional and roll-maintenance changes.

### Cited Findings
- **HB 458.** It amended ORC 3509.06, in effect April 7, 2023. — [ORC 3509.06](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3509.06)
- **Grace period history (10 days to 4 to 0).** — [Statehouse News Bureau](https://www.statenews.org/2025-11-19/ohio-lawmakers-vote-to-eliminate-the-four-day-grace-period-for-mail-in-ballots)
- **Drop box limit.** Current text of ORC 3509.05(C)(3). — [ORC 3509.05](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3509.05)
- **Provisional cure.** Four days, current text (SB 293 version). — [ORC 3505.181](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3505.181)

### Gaps
- That HB 458 specifically introduced the photo-ID requirement and cut the cure window from 7 days is background knowledge. I confirmed only the current statute texts, not the HB 458 bill text.

## 4. Historical election nights, 2018–2024

### Takeaway
Ohio is called on election night, usually 11 pm to after midnight ET, with about 90–95% of the vote in. The early Democratic lead from the absentee dump typically flips to the Republican by ~9:30 pm ET in high-early-vote years.

### Cited Findings
- **2018 Sen (Brown D vs. Renacci R).** Projected about 8:23 pm ET, the first Ohio race called on air. Brown won about 51–47.
  - [The Lantern live log](https://www.thelantern.com/?p=161947)
  - [Kent Stater](https://kentstater.com/14735/uncategorized/dewine-wins-governors-race-gop-sweeps-top-ohio-offices)
  - [Ironton Tribune](https://irontontribune.com/?p=391108)
- **2018 Gov (DeWine R vs. Cordray D).**
  - Polls had the race about tied.
  - Projected for DeWine around 11:14 pm ET at 51–46 with 94% of precincts in.
  - Cordray conceded about 11:30 pm.
  - Final lead was about 4.7 pts, roughly 200k votes, by the end of the night.
  - Sources: [The Lantern](https://www.thelantern.com/?p=161947); [WOSU](https://www.wosu.org/news/2018-11-06/mike-dewine-wins-ohio-governor-race)
- **2020 Pres.**
  - First votes reported 7:39 pm ET.
  - Biden took a "sizable" early lead that eroded over about 2 hours; Trump overtook him about 9:30 pm ET.
  - About 95% of the vote was in by midnight.
  - AP called Ohio at 12:19 am ET Wed Nov 4. Trump won 53–45.
  - Source: [AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day)
- **2022 Sen (Vance R vs. Ryan D).**
  - Ryan had a sizable early lead; Vance overtook him about 9:30 pm ET.
  - Sources: [AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day); [Dayton Daily News headline "Ryan takes early lead ... with absentee results coming in"](https://www.daytondailynews.com/local/ryan-takes-early-lead-in-us-senate-race-with-absentee-results-coming-in/OE5WKV4JIJF5LJNQVVN4NTXOMY/)
  - One local report puts the AP call "around 11:14 pm" with under 94% counted. The time zone was unstated and this is a single source. — [Carroll News](https://carrollnews.org/213635/world/vance-defeats-ryan-for-us-senate/)
  - Vance won 80 of 88 counties. Unofficial result was about 53.5–46.5 with 98% of precincts in.
- **2024 Sen (Moreno R vs. Brown D).**
  - AP called it at 11:28 pm ET Nov 5, with over 90% of the estimated vote in.
  - At the call Brown needed 71.9% of the remaining ballots and was not reaching that in any county.
  - Moreno was narrowly ahead in Cincinnati–Dayton. Brown led Cleveland and Columbus, but by less than in 2018.
  - Source: [AP "Why AP called" via ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/politics/2024/11/06/why-ap-called-the-ohio-senate-race-for-bernie-moreno/)
  - **Margin conflict.** The AP article says both "nearly 5-point lead" and "4 percentage-point lead". At 92% counted, Moreno led 50.6–46.0 (2,667,385 to 2,428,433). — [Tribune-Democrat / AP race call](https://www.tribtoday.com/news/2024/11/ap-race-call-republican-bernie-moreno-wins-election-to-u-s-senate-from-ohio-beating-incumbent-sherrod-brown/)
  - Bricker Graydon gives the final as 50.2–46.4. — [Bricker Graydon](https://www.bricker.com/insights/publications/2024-general-election-results-and-analysis)
  - Brown had "a marginal lead early on" before Moreno overtook him; this is a Mahoning Valley outlet's account. — [WFMJ](https://www.wfmj.com/story/51749159/decision-2024-us-senate-race-in-ohio)
- **2023 Issue 1 (abortion amendment).** AP called it shortly after 9 pm ET. It passed 56.35% with 99%+ of precincts in. — [Ohio Capital Journal via Stateline](https://stateline.org/2023/11/07/ohio-voters-pass-constitutional-amendment-to-protect-abortion-and-reproductive-rights/)

### Inferences
- **How long a lead takes to hold.** A lopsided race (2018 Sen, 2023 Issue 1) is called within ~1–1.5 h of poll close. A 4–8 pt race is called ~11 pm–12:30 am ET. A race under about 2 pts could go past election night.
- **Early lead and red shift.** Democrats lead early and Republicans overtake by about 9:30 pm ET when Election Day vote is about 50%+ of the total. If a Democrat wins in 2026, the early lead should hold or narrow rather than flip.

### Gaps
- I did not find a quantified size of the early Democratic lead (pts at 8 pm vs. final) for any year.
- I did not find a 2022 governor (DeWine–Whaley) call time; it was a blowout and presumably called early.
- I found no verified 2018 AP-specific call timestamps; The Lantern's times are media projections.
- Percent counted by midnight is verified only for 2020.

## 5. Partisan lean by vote method (mail vs. early in-person vs. Election Day), 2022/2024, plus 2026 early data

### Takeaway
Pre-election votes lean Democratic relative to Election Day (AP), but the split by method and party is poorly documented. In the 2024 general, more Republicans than Democrats (by primary-based affiliation) voted early, and unaffiliated voters were the majority. 2026 early voting so far looks markedly more Democratic than 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **AP characterization.** Mail and early in-person votes "have tended to favor Democrats". — [AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-ohio-on-election-day)
- **2024 general early vote by affiliation.**
  - More than 1.37M early voters were unaffiliated.
  - 707,741 Republicans vs. 447,656 Democrats voted early.
  - The figures are not split by method.
  - Source: [Ohio Capital Journal 2024-11-05](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2024/11/05/nearly-a-third-of-registered-ohio-voters-cast-their-ballots-early-in-the-2024-election/)
- **Ohio party labels.** Ohio has no party registration; affiliation comes from the voter's last primary ballot. — [CNN via KRDO](https://krdo.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/11/early-voting-figures-suggest-democratic-gains-in-ohios-most-trump-friendly-counties/)
- **March 2024 primary (cited as the general pattern).**
  - Mail returned: D 29,241 vs. R 28,835.
  - In person: R 42,875 vs. D 27,631.
  - Source: search summary; the source is unclear. Treat as weak evidence.
- **2026 early vote (CNN, ~Oct 11, 2026).**
  - The early electorate is about 27 pts more Democratic than at the same point in 2024.
  - In Trump +20 counties, the Republican early-vote lead shrank from 30 to about 5 pts.
  - Early in-person turnout is at 72% of its 2024 level, mail at 25%.
  - Statewide early turnout is at about 11% of the 2022 early total.
  - Source: [CNN via KRDO](https://krdo.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/11/early-voting-figures-suggest-democratic-gains-in-ohios-most-trump-friendly-counties/)
  - Caution: the article makes an odd comparison to Virginia, and the snapshot is very early.

### Inferences
- **The 2026 first dump.** It will likely be more Democratic than in 2024, given the CNN numbers, so the 2026 early Brown and Acton leads will likely look bigger than Brown's 2024 early lead. That makes the 7:30–9:00 pm ET numbers even less informative.
- **Low mail volume.** Mail at only 25% of its 2024 pace suggests a smaller mail share. That is consistent with Republican messaging and the new deadline, but this is speculative.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2022 or 2024 vote-method by candidate breakdown; the SoS canvass files may have it by county.
- I could not retrieve the 2026 SoS Oct 13 early-vote release (403).

## 6. Uncounted ballots after election night (late mail, provisionals), 2022 and 2024

### Takeaway
Provisionals are the main post-election-night pool. In 2024, 137,879 were outstanding statewide, about 2.4% of ballots cast. Late mail drops toward near zero in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 provisionals.**
  - 137,879 outstanding provisional ballots on election night.
  - Cure deadline Sat Nov 9.
  - LaRose warned that some contests, notably a northwest Ohio congressional race (OH-9), could change.
  - Sources: [Ohio SoS 2024-11-07 (snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2024-11-07); [Highland County Press](https://highlandcountypress.com/headlines/larose-encourages-every-eligible-ohioan-make-sure-their-vote-counts)
- **2024 total ballots cast.** 5,851,387, for 71.7% turnout. — [Highland County Press](https://www.highlandcountypress.com/headlines/larose-certifies-official-2024-general-election-results)
- **Late absentees in primaries.** 7,042 in the 2024 primary and 1,564 in the 2026 primary. — [Ohio SoS 2026-06-03 (snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2026-06-03)

### Inferences
- **Provisional share.** 137,879 / 5,851,387 ≈ 2.36% of ballots in 2024 (my calculation). Not all provisionals are counted, and they likely lean Democratic or young. That is a typical pattern I did not verify for Ohio.
- **Where a late shift could matter.** A statewide margin under about 1 pt could see a small Democratic shift in the canvass, but a flip of more than ~0.5 pt is unlikely.

### Gaps
- I did not find the 2024 general late-absentee count, the 2022 provisional and late-absentee counts, or the 2024 provisional acceptance rate.
