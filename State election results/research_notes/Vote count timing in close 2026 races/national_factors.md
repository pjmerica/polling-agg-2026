## 1. Watson v. RNC (post-Election-Day mail ballot receipt)

### Takeaway
SCOTUS decided Watson on June 29, 2026, 5-4 (Barrett). It held that the federal Election Day statutes do not bar states from counting mail ballots postmarked by Election Day that arrive later. Among the nine states, Alaska and Texas keep their grace periods. Kansas's grace period applies in 2026 only because a July 2026 preliminary injunction blocks its 2025 repeal. Ohio ended its grace period in 2025 for domestic voters.

### Cited Findings
- Decided June 29, 2026 (argued March 23, 2026), No. 24-1260, by a 5-4 vote — [Wikipedia: Watson v. RNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee)
  - The majority was Barrett, joined by Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson.
  - Alito dissented, joined by Thomas and Gorsuch. Kavanaugh joined the dissent except Parts II-C-2 and III.
- Holding: "the election-day statutes do not set a deadline for ballot receipt." Federal law governs when ballots must be cast, while "state law dictates when they must be received." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee)
- Procedural history: the Fifth Circuit (Oct 2024) had held that federal law requires receipt by Election Day, and SCOTUS reversed. Cert was granted in November 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee)
- Barrett noted that the statutes set no deadline for counting or certifying results — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee)
- NCSL Table 11 summary: "Watson v. RNC (June 29, 2026) ... federal law does not preempt state policies allowing post-Election Day receipt of postmarked ballots. Existing state deadlines remain in place." — [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots) (fetched 2026-10-21; no page-update date was returned)
- Domestic receipt deadlines from NCSL Table 11 for the nine states (fetched 2026-10-21):

  | State | Domestic receipt deadline |
  |---|---|
  | Alaska | Within 10 days if postmarked by Election Day |
  | Georgia | Close of polls |
  | Iowa | Before close of polls |
  | Kansas | 3rd day after the election if postmarked by Election Day (statute temporarily enjoined, see below) |
  | Maine | Close of polls |
  | Michigan | Close of polls |
  | Nebraska | Close of polls |
  | Ohio | Close of polls for most ballots |
  | Texas | 5 p.m. the day after the election if postmarked by Election Day |

  Source: [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)
- UOCAVA (military and overseas) windows from the same table:

  |---|---|

  | Iowa | Noon the Monday after the election, if postmarked by the day before Election Day |
  | Michigan | 6 days if postmarked by Election Day |

- Kansas: 2025 SB 4 required receipt by 7 p.m. on Election Day. A July 2026 preliminary injunction blocks it, so under the injunction, postmarked ballots received within three days can be counted — [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)
- Ohio: SB 293 (2025) ended the four-day post-election grace period for most absentee ballots. Military and overseas ballots keep it — [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)
- Other 2025-26 changes outside the nine states:
  - Utah HB 300 and North Dakota HB 1165 ended their grace periods.
  - Virginia moves its grace deadline effective Jan 1, 2027.
  - Mississippi HB 908 and Maryland SB 949 are contingency laws in case federal law had barred late receipt.

  Source: [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)
- Count of grace-period states: the NCSL text says 15 states. Its table lists 14 (AK, IL, KS, MD, MA, MS, NV, NJ, NY, OR, TX, VA, WA, WV) plus territories and DC — [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)
  - R Street says Mississippi "and 13 other states" — [R Street](https://www.rstreet.org/commentary/what-watson-v-rnc-means-for-2026-midterms/)
  - Brennan Center gives a broader "30 states and DC" figure, which likely counts UOCAVA-only or other grace rules — [Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/brennan-center-reacts-supreme-court-ruling-watson-v-republican-national)
- Reason (Aug 25, 2026) says legal fights over mail ballots continue in the lower courts — [Reason](https://reason.com/2026/08/25/another-supreme-court-showdown-over-mail-in-ballots/)

### Inferences
- For Nov 2026, late-arriving domestic mail ballots can still be added after election night in Alaska (up to 10 days, which is why Alaska results move for 1-2 weeks), Texas (through 5 p.m. Nov 4) and Kansas (through Nov 6, if the injunction holds).
- Georgia, Iowa, Maine, Michigan, Nebraska and Ohio accept only UOCAVA ballots after Election Day, which is a small volume.
- Watson removes the federal-preemption threat that EO 14248 Sec. 7 relied on (see section 2).

## 2. Executive orders on elections (EO 14248, March 2025; EO 14399, March 2026) and the USPS ballot rule

### Takeaway
EO 14248's citizenship-proof provisions are permanently enjoined (D. Mass.), and its ballot-receipt section (Sec. 7) is undercut by Watson. The March 31, 2026 EO 14399 has these core elements:
- a federal "State Citizenship List";
- a rule that USPS may deliver mail ballots only to listed voters;
- a USPS ballot-mail rule.

On Sept 14, 2026, SCOTUS left in place a lower-court freeze, with only Alito and Thomas dissenting. Votebeat therefore reports the restrictions cannot take effect for Nov 3, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **EO 14248 citizenship provisions:** D. Mass. permanently enjoined Secs. 2(d) and 3(d) (documentary proof of citizenship on registration and absentee applications) as violating separation of powers — [Organ Mountain News](https://www.organmountainnews.com/torrez-trump-election-executive-order-permanent-injunction-2026/); [CA AG, June 24, 2026 summary-judgment order (PDF)](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/2026-06-24%20-%20MSJ%20Mem.%20and%20Order.pdf)
- **EO 14248 ballot receipt (Secs. 7(a), 7(b)):**
  - The June 24, 2026 order (Judge Casper) largely ruled for the state coalition but granted the government summary judgment on Wisconsin's claims — [CA AG PDF](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/2026-06-24%20-%20MSJ%20Mem.%20and%20Order.pdf)
  - In June 2025, D. Mass. had preliminarily enjoined enforcement against 19 states' post-Election-Day receipt deadlines — [Wikipedia: Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee)
  - Washington and Oregon won a ruling on Jan 9, 2026 blocking the challenged parts — [Democracy Docket case page](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/washington-trump-election-integrity-executive-order-challenge/)
- **EO 14399** "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections," signed March 31, 2026, 91 FR 17125 (Apr 3, 2026) — [Wikipedia: EO 14399](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14399)
  - DHS and SSA build a "State Citizenship List."
  - "the USPS shall not transmit mail-in or absentee ballots from any individual unless" the voter is on the list.
  - USPS is to make rules on ballot mail (official election-mail marking, tracking).
- About two dozen Democratic-led states sued in April 2026 — [Al Jazeera, Apr 3, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/3/two-dozen-democrat-led-states-sue-trump-over-mail-in-ballot-limits)
- **Litigation timeline:**
  - June 2026: Judge Indira Talwani (D. Mass.) found key provisions unconstitutional and enjoined them — [Votebeat, Sept 15, 2026](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/09/15/trump-mail-voting-executive-order-supreme-court-decision-2026-election/)
  - Aug 24, 2026: SCOTUS (6-3) lifted Talwani's preliminary injunction as premature because the USPS rule was not final. It also granted a similar request from Alabama and 11 states. The government told the Court USPS "will not refuse to transmit ballots" — [NBC, Aug 24, 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-hands-preliminary-win-trump-mail-ballots-executive-order-rcna590120)
  - USPS issued a final rule on Friday, Aug 21, 2026. It requires a uniform ballot-envelope style and a state-supplied list of voters eligible to receive mail ballots — [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-hands-preliminary-win-trump-mail-ballots-executive-order-rcna590120); [PBS, Aug 26, 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/democratic-states-file-new-lawsuit-seeking-to-block-trumps-order-limiting-mail-voting-in-midterms)
    - Per Votebeat, the rule adds unique barcodes and an online federal list portal that was not yet available.
  - Aug 26, 2026: Talwani lifted her other order, saying she was "compelled" to. The states filed a new suit against the USPS rule, and a separate DC suit (LWV, filed May) was pending — [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/democratic-states-file-new-lawsuit-seeking-to-block-trumps-order-limiting-mail-voting-in-midterms)
  - Talwani then put the USPS plan on hold again. On Sept 14, 2026, SCOTUS issued a brief unsigned order keeping that freeze, with Alito and Thomas dissenting — [Votebeat, Sept 15, 2026](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/09/15/trump-mail-voting-executive-order-supreme-court-decision-2026-election/)
    - The Court said "The Government is unlikely to succeed on the merits."
    - Kavanaugh concurred, saying there is "at least a fair prospect" the rule is within USPS authority but officials lack time to implement it before the election.
    - Votebeat: mail voting "will largely work as it has in past elections."
- **Conflicting report:** ABC said a SCOTUS ruling let parts of the order proceed, while the list-verification provision "remains blocked in a different legal case" — [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/usps-makes-new-rules-mail-ballots/story?id=135879673)
  - This likely describes the Aug 24 order, which predates the Sept 14 ruling.
- DOJ asked SCOTUS to let the USPS action proceed before the midterms — [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/justice-department-usps-supreme-court-action-mail-ballots-elections-rcna595924)
  - This is probably the application decided Sept 14, but the date was not confirmed.

### Inferences
- As of Oct 21, the most recent fetched development (Sept 14) means no USPS list or envelope mandate applies on Nov 3, 2026. The nine states' mail ballots move under pre-existing rules.
- The indirect effect is behavioral. Marist found Democratic confidence in on-time USPS delivery fell from 86% (2024) to 60% (Sept 2026), and NBC/Penn data show Democratic mail requests dropped (section 5). That could shrink the Democratic lean of mail ballots and so the size of mirages.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm any developments between Sept 15 and Oct 21, 2026 (new rulings, USPS actions).
- I could not verify any separate executive action on voting machines (e.g., the August 2025 announced push against mail ballots and machines). I found no source showing such an order in force.

## 3. USPS postmark clarification (DMM 608.11, effective Dec 24, 2025)

### Takeaway
USPS says a postmark "does not necessarily indicate the first day that the Postal Service had possession of the mailpiece." This matters for Alaska, Texas and Kansas (postmark-based grace periods). Voters can get a free manual postmark at a retail counter.

### Cited Findings
- The rule took effect Dec 24, 2025 and states "the postmark date does not necessarily indicate the first day that the Postal Service had possession of the mailpiece" — [Stateline, Dec 30, 2025](https://stateline.org/2025/12/30/usps-says-mail-in-ballots-might-not-get-postmark-on-same-day-theyre-dropped-off/)
- Risk: in the 14 grace-period states plus DC, a ballot mailed on time could be postmarked after Election Day and rejected — [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2025/12/30/usps-says-mail-in-ballots-might-not-get-postmark-on-same-day-theyre-dropped-off/)
- USPS says this is a clarification, not a practice change. It confirmed that nothing in DMM 608.11 changes ballot-mail treatment — [Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/update/heres-what-new-usps-rule-means-voting-mail)
- Voter guidance: request a free manual (hand-cancel) postmark at any post office, or buy a Certificate of Mailing as proof of the mailing date — [Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/update/heres-what-new-usps-rule-means-voting-mail)

### Inferences
- In AK, TX and KS, a few ballots dropped in collection boxes on Nov 2-3 could carry Nov 4+ postmarks. This is a likely source of post-election provisional and rejection disputes in very close races.
- In the other six states, postmarks are irrelevant for domestic voters.

### Gaps
- I found no state-specific 2026 USPS or election-office guidance for the nine states.

## 5. Race calling (AP, Decision Desk HQ) and red/blue mirage patterns

### Takeaway
AP doesn't call before a state's last polls close and doesn't "project." It declares a winner only when trailing candidates have no path, and it uses AP VoteCast (with NORC) to confirm calls in non-competitive races at poll close. In 2026, mail-voting habits are shifting: Democrats are requesting fewer mail ballots, which could shrink the familiar mirages.

### Cited Findings
- AP will not call a race before the last polls close in a jurisdiction, including in states where polls do not all close at once — [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/how-the-ap-calls-races-and-what-to-expect-on-election-night); [KIVI/AP explainer](https://www.kivitv.com/news/explainer-how-can-the-ap-call-races-right-as-polls-close)
  - AP "does not make projections" and declares a winner only when "there is no scenario that would allow the trailing candidates to close the gap."
- Calls at poll close are for uncontested or historically lopsided races, confirmed by AP VoteCast. VoteCast was developed with NORC at the University of Chicago to replace exit polls — [ABC17/AP](https://abc17news.com/?p=1029177); [Al Jazeera 2022](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/11/9/how-can-the-ap-call-us-midterm-races-right-as-polls-close)
- AP's 2022 midterm plan covered Senate, House and governor races with VoteCast, plus thousands of down-ballot races via state-based race callers — [Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/ap-announces-midterm-election-coverage-plans,240641)
- Mirage mechanics:
  - Blue shift: in-person Election Day votes are counted first and mail ballots later, so the Democratic share rises over the count — [Protect Democracy](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/the-blue-shift-and-red-mirage-in-election-results-explained/)
  - Blue mirage, then red shift: where pre-processed mail and early votes are released first, as in Georgia — [Forbes, 2024](https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2024/11/04/blue-mirage-and-red-mirage-explained-why-tomorrows-first-swing-state-vote-counts-may-be-misleading/)
- Michigan's expanded pre-processing was expected to make its red mirage less severe — [Axios, 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/11/05/trump-harris-2024-election-absentee-voting-red-mirage)
- Georgia reports fast, and its 2021 law shifted voters toward in-person early voting — [Forbes 2024](https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2024/11/04/blue-mirage-and-red-mirage-explained-why-tomorrows-first-swing-state-vote-counts-may-be-misleading/)
- NBC Decision Desk / Penn analysis (Oct 9, 2026) — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/early-vote-analysis-mail-ballot-concerns-are-changing-democrats-vote-rcna602489)
  - Georgia mail requests fell 52% in strong-Harris precincts and 39% in strong-Trump precincts, versus 2022.
  - Marist: Democratic confidence in USPS on-time delivery fell from 86% (2024) to 60% (Sept 2026).
  - More Democrats plan to vote in person.
  - Changing habits could make the red and blue "mirages" unfold differently.
- A California June 2, 2026 primary "red mirage" drew comment — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/mail-ballot-mirages-arent-fraud-but-mail-ballot-fraud-isnt-a-complete-myth-285992)

### Inferences
These are by state and come from the reporting-order rules in the table below. They are not from published 2026 analyses.

- **Georgia, Ohio, Iowa, Michigan, Texas, Nebraska, Kansas, Maine:** wherever pre-processed absentee and early votes are tabulated in advance, the first batches after poll close are likely early/mail-heavy. That gives a Democratic-leaning first drop where Democrats vote early or by mail more, then a Republican shift as Election Day precincts report.
- **Texas and Kansas (and Alaska):** late-arriving grace-period ballots add a small tail after election night.
- **Alaska:** election-night results are early-vote plus Election Day, and mail ballots received over the following 10 days (15 for UOCAVA) shift the count. Alaska's ranked-choice tabulation happens later. Statewide races are not decided on election night if no one has 50%+.
- **Maine:** ranked-choice tabulation for congressional races (if no majority) happens centrally days later.
- The 2026 Democratic move away from mail may shrink these effects.
