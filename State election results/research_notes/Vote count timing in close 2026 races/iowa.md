## 2. 2026 rules: deadlines, absentee processing, counting, canvass, recount

### Takeaway
Polls close at 8:00 p.m. CT (9 p.m. ET). Absentee ballots must be *received* by 8 p.m. on Election Day; there is no postmark grace for ordinary voters.

Each county counts all absentees in one central "special precinct" board:
- The board must convene by 9 a.m. on Election Day.
- It may start counting the day before.
- It is staffed to finish by 10 p.m.
- It is barred by law from releasing *any* absentee tally until its count is complete.

The 2026 calendar:
- Provisional ID/residency proof is due by noon Mon Nov 9.
- County canvass is Tue Nov 10.
- A recount request is due 5 p.m. the day after the canvass.
- State canvass is by Mon Nov 30.
- A state-ordered recount for statewide or federal office is available only if the margin is under 0.15%.

### Cited Findings

**Polls and early voting**
- Polls open at 7:00 a.m. "The polling places shall be closed at 8:00 p.m. for all elections." — [Iowa Code §49.73](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/49.73.pdf)
- In-person absentee (early) voting: "Not more than twenty days before the date of the primary election or the general election, the commissioner shall provide facilities for absentee voting in person." — [Iowa Code §53.10(1)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/53.10.pdf)
- That window is Oct 14 through Nov 2, 2026. — [Marion County Auditor 2026 key dates (PDF)](https://mintchiplab.marioncountyiowa.gov/assets/marion_county/files/auditor/2026_general_election_key_dates_33668.pdf); [US Vote Foundation](https://www.usvotefoundation.org/iowa-election-dates-and-deadlines)

**Absentee requests and returns**
- Absentee requests opened Aug 25, 2026 (70 days out). — [Iowa Public Radio, Aug 26, 2026](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/political-news/2026-08-26/absentee-ballot-requests-begin-in-iowa-for-nov-3-election)
- The mailed-request deadline was 5 p.m. Oct 19 (15 days out). — same IPR article
- Auditors could begin mailing ballots Oct 14. — same IPR article
- Ballots must reach the auditor by 8 p.m. Nov 3. — same IPR article
- A voter who has not returned a mailed ballot can surrender it at the polls and vote a regular ballot, or vote provisionally. — same IPR article
- The mailed-request deadline is now 15 days before the election (formerly 11 days, and 10 for generals). Satellite early-voting sites now require a petition of 100 signatures. — [Ringgold County election-law-changes notice](https://ringgoldcountyelections.iowa.gov/news/election_law_changes_for_iowa_voters/)
- Receipt rule: "In order for the ballot to be counted, the return envelope must be received in the commissioner's office before the polls close on election day." — [Iowa Code §53.17(2)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/53.17.pdf)
- Drop boxes:
  - At most one per county, at or adjacent to the auditor's office.
  - Under 24/7 video surveillance and emptied at least four times a day.
  - Closed "immediately upon the closure of polls." — [Iowa Code §53.17(1)(c)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/53.17.pdf)

**Absentee counting board ([Iowa Code §53.23](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/53.23.pdf))**
- Staffing: each county's absentee/special-voters precinct board must be staffed "sufficient to complete the counting of absentee ballots by 10:00 p.m. on election day." — §53.23(1)
- One board per county: the board counts all absentee ballots for all precincts in the county. — §53.23(2)
- Convening time: "The commissioner shall set a convening time for the board of no later than 9:00 a.m. on election day." — §53.23(3)(a)
  - This paragraph was amended by 2025 Acts ch 123, which is HF 954. Ballotpedia describes HF 954 as requiring absentee counting to begin no later than 9 a.m. on Election Day. — [Ballotpedia News, Jun 4, 2025](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/06/04/iowa-gov-reynolds-signs-bill-banning-rcv/)
- Pre-processing: the auditor may have the board meet the day before (Mon Nov 2) to review affidavits. If needed because of volume, the board may open affidavit envelopes and remove the secrecy envelopes, with party observers present. — §53.23(3)(b)
- Early counting: "The commissioner may convene the special precinct election board on the day before the election to begin counting absentee ballots."
  - This is *mandatory* if the county's absentee count was not finished by 10 p.m. at the preceding general election.
  - "The board shall not release the results of its tabulation pursuant to this paragraph until the count is completed on election day." — §53.23(3)(c)
- Release rule: "The special precinct election board shall not release the results of its tabulation on election day until all of the ballots it is required to count on that day have been counted." — §53.23(6)
- Secrecy: it is unlawful for anyone in the counting room to communicate count progress "at any time before the polls are closed." — §53.23(4)

**Election-night reporting**
- Precinct officials announce results and deliver them in person to the auditor. Electronic transmission is allowed only after a written results report is produced. The auditor must remain on duty until every polling place reports. — [Iowa Code §50.11](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.11.pdf)
- Unofficial statewide canvass: the Secretary of State conducts an unofficial canvass on election night. County auditors "periodically provide election results to the state commissioner ... as the precincts in the county report." — [Iowa Code §50.15A](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.15A.pdf)
  - An auditor who will not have all precincts in before closing the office must report the most complete results available and explain in writing why the rest are missing. — same section
  - The state "shall periodically make the reports of the results available to the public." — same section

**Provisional ballots**
- The per-precinct count of provisional ballots must be public no later than 9:00 a.m. on the second day after the election (Thu Nov 5, 2026). — [Iowa Code §50.20](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.20.pdf)
- The special precinct board reconvenes "not earlier than noon on the second day following" the election (Thu Nov 5, 2026). This subsection was amended by 2025 Acts ch 122. — [Iowa Code §50.21](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.21.pdf)
- Cure deadline: provisional voters must supply ID or residency proof at the polls before close, or to the auditor "not later than noon on the Monday following the election" (Mon Nov 9, 2026). — [Iowa Code §49.81(6)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/49.81.pdf)
- Uncured ballots are rejected. Accepted provisionals are counted like absentees. The board also canvasses any absentees lawfully received after polls closed and submits its tally before the supervisors' canvass ends. — [Iowa Code §50.22](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.22.pdf)

**Canvass**
- County canvass: the board of supervisors canvasses "on the first Tuesday after the day of each election" (Tue Nov 10, 2026). — [Iowa Code §50.24(1)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.24.pdf)
- State canvass: "Not later than twenty-seven days after the day of the election" the Secretary of State presents abstracts to the state board of canvassers (deadline Mon Nov 30, 2026). — [Iowa Code §50.38](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.38.pdf)

**Recount ([Iowa Code §50.48](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.48.pdf))**
- Request deadline: a written request is due by 5:00 p.m. on the day after the county canvass. — §50.48(1)
- Threshold: for statewide or federal office, the state commissioner orders a recount if the margin "is less than fifteen hundredths of one percent." — §50.48(1)(c)
- Start: the state must order the start within 7 days of receiving the request. — §50.48(1)(c)
- Board: the county auditor and staff, with equal numbers from each party if tally workers are used. — §50.48(2)
- Timing: the board convenes by 9 a.m. on the 7th day after the request. The text I extracted says the recount board must file its report "not later than the eighteenth day following the commissioner's receipt of a request." — §50.48
- HF 928 (signed June 2, 2025) set the 0.15% threshold for statewide/federal races (1% or 50 votes for others) and moved recount boards to auditors and staff. Previously each candidate named one member and the two agreed on a third. — [Iowa Capital Dispatch via Yahoo, Jun 2, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/gov-kim-reynolds-signs-bills-194737602.html)

**HF 954 (2025)**
- Citizenship: allows the SoS to verify rolls with federal/state agencies, mark registrations "unconfirmed," and permits citizenship challenges at the polls. A challenged voter votes provisionally if the challenge stands. — [same Yahoo/Capital Dispatch article](https://www.yahoo.com/news/gov-kim-reynolds-signs-bills-194737602.html)
- Other provisions: it also bans ranked-choice voting, authorizes e-poll books and sets the 9 a.m. absentee-count start. — [Ballotpedia News](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/06/04/iowa-gov-reynolds-signs-bill-banning-rcv/)

### Inferences
- **The absentee batch arrives early and all at once, not incrementally.** §53.23(6) forbids releasing partial absentee tallies. So in each county the absentee/early vote appears as a single lump, usually early in the evening (some counties may count Monday and be ready right at 8 p.m.). Large counties (Polk, Linn, Johnson, Scott) may post theirs later. The 10 p.m. statutory target means essentially every county's absentee lump should be in by about 10–11 p.m. CT.
- **Under 0.15%, a statewide recount runs into late November.** 0.15% of roughly 1.2–1.3M midterm votes is about 1,800–2,000 votes. Request by Nov 11, start ordered by about Nov 18, and the board's 18-day completion limit would end around Nov 29. That is close to the Nov 30 state-canvass deadline.
- **Holiday conflict.** The recount-request deadline falls on Wed Nov 11, 2026, which is Veterans Day. §50.24 references §4.1(34) for holidays only regarding the canvass Tuesday, so whether the request deadline shifts is unclear.
- **Post-election changes are small.** Mail ballots must be received by close of polls, and the provisional cure window closes Nov 9. So the post-election vote change is only provisionals (historically small in Iowa) plus overseas/military items, if any. A modest lead at the end of election night is very likely to hold.

### Gaps
- **Recount timing details.** The "eighteenth day" recount completion and "convene by the seventh day" language come from my PDF text extraction of §50.48. I did not confirm which subsection they sit in, or whether different limits apply to state-ordered statewide recounts. A related extract line ("report shall be filed not later than the fourteenth day after the election") appears to be the presidential-elector provision.
- **HF 928 citation.** The 2026 Code history line I extracted for §50.48 did not visibly list a 2025 Act, though the text matches HF 928's described content. The extraction may simply have been truncated; check the Code if precision matters.
- **Overseas/military ballots.** I did not verify whether UOCAVA ballots get a post-Election-Day receipt window (§53.17(3)/§50.22(4) refer to absentees "received after the polls closed in accordance with section 53.17").
- **No 2026 official calendar.** The Iowa SoS 2026 election calendar was not retrieved, so the Nov 10 and Nov 30 dates are computed from statute, not taken from an SoS calendar.

## 3. Reporting units, results site, cadence and order

### Takeaway
There are 99 county auditors; each county has one central absentee precinct plus election-day precincts. Results feed the SoS unofficial canvass on election night. First votes typically post about 10–15 minutes after the 8 p.m. close. Most of the vote has historically been in by 11 p.m. CT. Polk (Des Moines) reports large Democratic margins early; rural counties finish late and pull the total toward Republicans.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory flow: precinct to county auditor (§50.11), then auditor to SoS periodically as precincts report (§50.15A). — [§50.11](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.11.pdf), [§50.15A](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/50.15A.pdf)
- Official site: the SoS results portal electionresults.iowa.gov loads as a "List Elections" app. I could not read the vendor or cadence. — [electionresults.iowa.gov](https://electionresults.iowa.gov/)
- 2020, per AP: first votes reported at 10:11 p.m. ET (9:11 p.m. CT). About 82% of total votes were reported by midnight ET (11 p.m. CT). — [AP Decision Notes: What to expect in Iowa (Oct 25, 2024), via SRN](https://srnnews.com/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-iowa-on-election-day/)
- Polk County reports early. In 2018 it had 153 of 177 precincts in by 9:30 p.m. CT and 170 of 177 by 9:45 p.m. — [Bleeding Heartland live thread, Nov 6, 2018](https://www.bleedingheartland.com/2018/11/06/iowa-and-national-2018-election-results-thread/)
- Rural counties finish last. At 11:00 p.m. CT in 2018, Reynolds overtook Hubbell "with mostly rural counties left to report." — same Bleeding Heartland thread
- Polk 2019 (municipal): counting began at 8 p.m. and finished about 5:30–6 a.m. Wednesday. This was an off-year municipal election, so it is a weak analog. — [WHO13](https://digital-release.who13.com/?p=713830)
- Johnson County (Iowa City, strongly D) ran its provisional-ballot meeting on Tue Nov 12, 2024 and reported its final count Wed Nov 13. — [KCRG, Nov 13, 2024](https://www.kcrg.com/2024/11/14/results-1st-congressional-district-race-expected-wednesday-night-counties-finish-canvassing)

### Inferences
- **Expected order on Nov 3, 2026 (CT):**
  - 8:00–8:30 p.m.: absentee lumps from counties that pre-counted or count fast. These lean D (or near even, per 2024), so Turek is likely to lead or run strong in early totals.
  - 8:30–10 p.m.: urban precincts (Polk, Linn, Johnson, Story, Scott, Black Hawk) report heavily D margins.
  - 10–11:30 p.m.: rural election-day precincts arrive, moving totals toward Hinson.
- **Likely "red shift."** Iowa's early-reported vote has leaned D relative to the final result, both in the 2018 Hubbell lead and in the AP data on early-vote share. If Hinson wins, a Turek early lead that collapses around 10–11 p.m. CT is the historical pattern.
- **Calling a close race.** About 80–85% of the vote is typically in by 11 p.m. CT. A race inside about 2 points will likely remain uncalled until rural counties close out after midnight. Anything inside about 0.5% may wait for the Nov 10 canvass.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain a 2022 or 2024 time series of percent reported by hour.
- I did not confirm which counties pre-count absentees on Monday in 2026, or whether Polk, Linn or Scott missed the 10 p.m. absentee target in 2024. Missing it would *require* Monday counting in 2026 under §53.23(3)(c).
- The current results-site vendor (Clarity/Scytl vs. other) is unverified.

- **2018 swing magnitude:** Hubbell +about 45,000 at 10:20 p.m. CT, final Reynolds +about 39,600. That is an intra-night swing of about 85,000 votes, roughly 6–7 points of margin, driven by reporting order (Polk and urban first, rural last) rather than vote mode alone.

## 5. Absentee vs. Election Day share and partisan lean

### Takeaway
Early/absentee share swings with the cycle:
- 2020: about 59%, the pandemic year with all voters mailed request forms.
- 2022: about 30%.
- 2024: roughly 38% (inferred).

Absentee voters historically skewed Democratic, but in 2024 Republicans out-voted Democrats early (268,629 to 257,634 by party registration). So the "absentee = blue" assumption is weaker than in 2018–2022.

### Cited Findings
- Early share by year, per AP: about 59% of the total vote in 2020 and about 30% in 2022. — [AP via SRN](https://srnnews.com/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-iowa-on-election-day/)
- 2022 split: 365,690 early-by-mail (30%) and 850,956 election-day (70%). — [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2022-elections/iowa-senate-results/)
- 2024 absentees received as of Nov 4: 634,836 total — Democrats 243,902, Republicans 252,139, no-party 135,024. — [Bleeding Heartland tracker](https://www.bleedingheartland.com/2024/10/18/iowa-absentee-ballot-numbers-in-the-2024-general-election/)
- The same tracker notes Iowa Democrats have historically been "far more likely than Republicans to cast early ballots." — same Bleeding Heartland tracker
