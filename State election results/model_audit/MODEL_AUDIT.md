# Model audit, 2026-10-21: what the forecast's inputs got wrong, and what was fixed

*Scope: everything the polling model sees for the competitive Governor/Senate races (AK-Sen, GA-Gov,
OH-Gov, OH-Sen special, KS-Sen, TX-Sen, NE-Sen, ME-Sen, IA-Sen, MI-Sen), plus the training data
behind it. Model repo: `Documents\Polling prediction model` (GitHub `pjmerica/Polls-prediction-model`);
poll feeds: polling-agg `data/raw/{nyt_polls,wikipedia_polls}.csv`. Every before/after table below is
generated by `model_audit/scripts/` from the model's own code, not typed.*

## Summary

- **The published forecast was reproduced exactly first:** 3,062 poll rows, 173 races, generic ballot
  D+8.72; Alaska 50.6%, Georgia 55.2%, Michigan 76.3%. Then every poll row and every model input for the 10 races was
  dumped and checked against the raw feeds and primary sources.
- **The biggest problems were in how polls enter the model, not in the polls themselves:**
  1. the same survey counted twice;
  2. a candidate's number taken from a different question than the opponent's;
  3. registered-voter versions used instead of likely-voter;
  4. one-sided questions;
  5. polls from the previous cycle;
  6. month-only dates;
  7. bipartisan polls labelled as party-sponsored.
- **Candidate and training-data errors fixed:**
  - two experience-level misclassifications;
  - two wrong party labels in the training polls;
  - six independents coded inconsistently with how the model treats Dan Osborn;
  - the ranked-choice finalist rule failing in Alaska's governor race.
- **Effect, before the retrain:**
  - Michigan Senate El-Sayed 76.3% → 70.0%;
  - Maine Jackson 69.1% → 72.1%;
  - Ohio Senate Brown 64.2% → 67.1%;
  - Texas Talarico 64.9% → 62.7%;
  - Ohio Governor Acton 57.4% → 55.3%;
  - Nebraska Ricketts 68.1% → 67.1%;
  - Alaska, Georgia, Iowa and Kansas within ±0.2.

  Across all 173 races, 18 moved 2+ points and none changed favorite.
- **After retraining on the corrected training data (published 2026-10-22):**
  - Held-out accuracy is unchanged: win Brier 0.064, 87.8% of races called right (was 0.064 / 88.3%); margin error 5.46 points (was 5.47).
  - The retune picked different settings (trees two levels deep instead of one). On the tuning cycles the two settings are tied (AUC 0.9522 vs 0.9519), and they are also tied on 2018–2024. But they disagree by up to 15 points on 2026 races (Florida 22nd: 78.6% vs 64.1%).
  - Retrained with the old settings, the forecast barely moves beyond the data fixes. Almost all of the movement below comes from the new settings (Part 5c).
  - Published, by your choice: Michigan El-Sayed 63.4%, Texas Talarico 55.0%, Ohio Senate Brown 58.7%, Nebraska Ricketts 62.2%, Iowa Hinson 68.2%, Maine Jackson 71.6%. Across the slate, 80 races moved 2+ points and five House favorites flipped from D to R (AZ-6, CO-5, IA-3, MI-7, MI-10).
  - Three more governor races are now at 75% or less: Nevada (Lombardo 69.5%), Oregon (Kotek 70.1%) and Texas (Abbott 74.0%).
- **Found but not changed** (modeling decisions, listed with recommendations in Part 3):
  - the poll window is different before and after 2018 in the training data;
  - "prior margin" mixes both Senate seats;
  - the PAC-share feature is acting as a party proxy in 2026;
  - the win-model tuner picks between near-tied settings by a margin far smaller than the settings' disagreement.

## Part 1. Poll-feed defects (what the live model was fed)

All fixes are in the model repo's shared code (`src/features.py`, used by training AND prediction, and
`src/predict.py`). One exception is #7, which is in polling-agg's Wikipedia scraper.

**1. The same survey counted twice.**
- **What happened:** NYT and Wikipedia file the same poll under different names, and NYT carries decimals where Wikipedia rounds. So the existing dedup (normalized name, exact numbers) missed:
  - "The New York Times/Siena University" vs "New York Times / Siena University";
  - Fox News polls as "Beacon Research/Shaw & Company Research" vs "Beacon Research (D)/ Shaw & Co. Research (R)";
  - "Cygnal" vs "Cygnal Political";
  - "YouGov" vs "CBS News / YouGov";
  - two Bowling Green State University spellings.
- **Scope:** the first pass found at least 43 surviving duplicate pairs in 23 of 173 races, including **every NYT/Siena poll** (19 pairs) and 8 Fox pairs.
- **Fixes:**
  - `norm_pollster` drops a leading "the" and the spacing around "/".
  - New `drop_cross_source_copies`: same race and end date, at least 2 shared candidates within 0.6 pts (rounding), and same sample size or one name containing the other.

**2. A candidate's number taken from a different question than the opponent's.**
- **What happened:** the model kept "the first row per candidate per survey," so a survey with several questions could pair numbers from different questions:
  - **Nebraska, Tavern Research 5/11:** Osborn 47 (from Osborn v Ricketts) against Ricketts 49 (from Ricketts v "Generic Democrat"). The real head-to-head was **Osborn 47, Ricketts 42**, a 7-point error.
  - **Ohio Senate, Bowling Green Feb 2025:** Husted's **45 against Tim Ryan**, who isn't running, sat next to Brown's 41.
  - **Mississippi Senate, Impact Research June 2025:** Hyde-Smith's 38 from one question paired with Colom from another.
- **Fix:** new `one_question_per_survey`, which keeps one whole question per survey. The chosen question:
  1. covers both of the race's leading candidates (ranked by number of surveys, then average, so a one-off hypothetical opponent can't outrank the nominee);
  2. then prefers likely voters, then registered voters, then voters, then adults;
  3. then the fuller ballot;
  4. then NYT before Wikipedia.

**3. Registered-voter versions used instead of likely-voter.**
- **What happened:** pollsters that publish both versions often had their RV numbers used, because the RV question came first in the feed:
  - Marist Michigan 10/18: RV El-Sayed 52–45 against LV 50–48;
  - Marist Ohio Senate 10/18: RV Brown 51–42 against LV 48–44;
  - Fox Iowa 9/28: RV Turek 50–46 against LV 49–47.
- **Effect:** RV samples lean Democratic this cycle, so this tilted the forecast toward Democrats wherever a poll had both versions.
- **Fix:** part of #2 (likely voters preferred, the convention 538 used for the training polls).

**4. One-sided questions.**
- **What happened:** questions where only one candidate's number survived (the other row went missing upstream, or belonged to a dead matchup), for example GQR's lone Rogers 42 in Michigan and Stratus's lone Paxton 39. The first pass found 113 such questions in 44 races; after #2 most resolve to complete questions.
- **Fix:** new `drop_one_sided_questions`. It drops 26 rows in the current feed.

**5. Polls from the previous cycle.**
- **Examples:**
  - Alaska Senate carried an **April 2023** Alaska Survey Research Peltola v Sullivan question, 1,288 days out and before Peltola's 2024 House race.
  - Nevada Governor carried a **May 2023** Vote TXT poll (Lombardo 51, Ford 30).
- **Context:** no training cycle reaches that far (the maximum is 1,020 days, in 2024).
- **Fix:** new `drop_out_of_cycle` drops polls ending on or before the previous general election. It applies in training and live, and drops 4 rows now.

**6. Month-only dates.**
- **What happened:** Wikipedia lists some polls as just "April 2026," which is parsed as the 1st, while NYT has the real end date. Nebraska's GBAO polls were each in the feed twice (4/1 + 4/20, 7/1 + 7/26).
- **Fix:** in `drop_cross_source_copies`, a survey dated the 1st that matches the same pollster's survey later that month is dropped as the imprecise copy.

**7. Bipartisan polls tagged as party-sponsored** (polling-agg, `scrapers/wikipedia_polls.py`).
- **What happened:** the scraper took a pair's trailing "(R)" or "(D)" as the sponsor, so bipartisan pairs were stored as sponsored:
  - Fox News ("Beacon Research (D)/ Shaw & Co. Research (R)") as Republican, 77 rows;
  - AARP's Fabrizio Ward (R)/Impact Research (D) as Democratic, 49 rows;
  - NBC's Hart (D)/Public Opinion Strategies (R) as Republican, 33 rows;
  - smaller pairs: GS Strategy/New River, Impact/National Research, Beacon/Cygnal, Healthier Colorado/Magellan.

  That fed the model's sponsor-split features (Democratic-sponsored / Republican-sponsored poll averages) the wrong way.
- **Fix:** a name tagged with both parties now has no sponsor. The 189 stored rows were re-tagged, and a regression test was added (`tests/test_wikipedia_polls.py`). Same-party pairs (GBAO (D)/Third Way (D)) keep their tag.

**8. Ranked-choice finalists picked from stale questions** (`rcv_final_round`).
- **What happened:** in ranked-choice races the model keeps only the final-round head-to-heads of the two expected finalists. It picked the finalists from every multi-candidate question in the cycle. Alaska Governor's early-2025 polls tested **Mary Peltola for governor**, so the rule chose Peltola and Kreiss-Tomkins, found no such head-to-head, and never switched the race to its Kreiss-Tomkins v Wilson head-to-heads.
- **Fix:** finalists come from the race's last 60 days of multi-candidate questions. Alaska Governor now uses its 5 final-round polls (Kreiss-Tomkins 51.6–55, Wilson 45–48.4).

## Part 2. Candidate and training-data errors (fixed)

**El-Sayed's experience level (MI-Sen, live).**
- **What was wrong:** "former Wayne County health director" matched the classifier's local-ELECTED list (`county ... health director` = 1). County health directors are appointed (0.5 under the 2026-10-14 rule).
- **Fix:** rule removed and the 2024/2026 rows recoded to 0.5.
- **Effect on Michigan:** none (the model doesn't split between 0.5 and 1 there). All of Michigan's move comes from the poll fixes.

**Scott Costello, IN-1 2020 (training).**
- **What was wrong:** "hospital behavioral health director" matched the appointed-health-director rule (0.5). It's a private job.
- **Fix:** the classifier now strips private-employer titles (hospital / clinic / company / nonprofit / foundation … director); the row is recoded to 0.

Only these 3 stored rows change under the two classifier edits (checked by re-classifying every stored descriptor with the old and new rules).

**Wrong party labels in the training polls** (`data/model_party_overrides_hist.csv`, applied in `build_candidate_table`):
- **Ronnie Musgrove**, Mississippi Senate special 2008: filed "other," but he was the **Democratic** nominee (Mississippi special-election ballots carry no party labels).
- **Shelley Sekula-Gibbs**, Texas 22nd District 2006: filed "other," but she was the **Republican** write-in after DeLay withdrew.

**Independents coded inconsistently with Osborn.**
- **The inconsistency:** since 2026-07-12 the live model puts an independent who is the de-facto challenger with no Democrat on the ballot in the Democratic slot (Osborn). The training polls had every such case as "other," including **Osborn himself in 2024**, whose results row was already on the Democratic line.
- **Now in the Democratic slot for training:**
  - Orman (KS-Sen 2014)
  - Walker (AK-Gov 2014, unity ticket)
  - McMullin (UT-Sen 2022)
  - Mund (ND-AL 2022)
  - Osborn (NE-Sen 2024)
  - Kidd (NV-2 2024)
- **Left as "other":** sitting independents Sanders and King (moving them breaks incumbency inputs; King's races also had a Democrat on the ballot) and Libertarian nominee Harrington (AR-Sen 2020).

In total, 8 overrides covering 106 training poll rows.

## Part 3. Training-data issues found but NOT changed (your call)

**1. The poll window differs by era.**
- **What the data shows:** "poll average" is the plain mean of all of a race's polls. In the training data that means:

  | Training years | Median poll age | Max poll age | Share older than 60 days |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1998–2016 (10 of 14 cycles, 538's pollster-ratings file) | 14–27 days | 61 days | about 0% |
  | 2018–2024 | 38–50 days | up to 1,020 days | 31–44% (8–17% older than 6 months) |
  | Live 2026 | — | — | like 2018–2024 |

  So the same feature means "the last two months" in most of the training data and "the whole campaign" in recent cycles and live.
- **Recommended** (your features-not-corrections rule): add same-window versions computed identically in every cycle, e.g. poll average / lead / count over the last 60 days. Retrain and keep them only if the ablation supports it.

**2. "Prior margin" mixes both Senate seats.**
- **What the data shows:** the code keys the prior result on (year, state, office, district), so for the Senate it's the most recent race for *either* seat. METHODOLOGY says "for that seat." As a result:
  - **Maine 2026** reads the 2024 King race: Republican 34.6 against the Democrat's 10.8, so R+23.8, when Collins won this seat in 2020 by 8.6.
  - **Alaska 2026** reads 2022 Murkowski–Tshibaka: R+32.7, against Sullivan's own 2020 margin of 12.7.
  - The **Ohio special** has no value at all (specials have no prior).
- **Effect now:** small (±0.06–0.12 on the model's internal score per race).
- **Recommended:** key the prior on the seat class (`office_seat_name` in the results files is "Class I/II/III"), give specials the seat's previous regular election, and test a "vs best opponent" margin for races where an independent took the non-Republican side.

**3. Share of money from PACs acts as a party proxy in 2026.**
- **What the data shows:** every Democrat in the 10 races (and Osborn) has 0.4–2.7% of receipts from PACs, and the feature costs each of them −0.29 on the model's internal score (about 7 points of probability). Republicans get +0.03.

  | Senate candidates with >$500K | Median PAC share, Democrats | Median PAC share, Republicans |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2018–2024 | 3.3% | 2.4% |
  | 2026 | 1.8% | 7.2% |

  So the feature now mostly re-encodes party and incumbency.
- **Recommended:** run an ablation with it removed (or replaced by a within-party relative measure).

**4. The win-model tuner's pick is a coin flip with large consequences.**
- **What the data shows:** the tuner scores 150 settings on the 1998–2016 cycles and keeps the highest AUC. After the data fixes it picked two-level trees (AUC 0.9522) over the one-level trees used before (0.9519). The old settings have the better Brier on the same cycles (0.0873 vs 0.0876).
- **Other measures:** on 2018–2024 the two are also tied (Brier 0.0634 vs 0.0635). Yet they differ by up to 15 points on 2026 races, mostly challengers with small poll leads against incumbents (Part 5c).
- **Why:** AUC only ranks candidates and ignores calibration, but the forecast publishes probabilities.
- **Recommended:**
  - select on Brier or log loss;
  - average the top few settings, as the margin tuner already does since 2026-10-10.

  Either way, a retrain would no longer swing races on a tie. You chose to publish the tuner's pick for now (2026-10-22).

**5. Other notes.**
- Alaska's top-four primary result (Peltola 49.5–41.4) isn't used by the model; primary-margin features are blank for top-four and jungle states.
- Ranked-choice margin labels already use the final round, matching the head-to-head polls, so there's no issue there.
- The 2018–2024 training polls include pre-nomination matchups of the eventual nominees (538's "hypothetical" flag), the same as live, so that's consistent.
