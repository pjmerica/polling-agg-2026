# Close 2026 Races Finish on Staggered Clocks

> **Checked 2026-10-21:** several statements here were corrected after re-checking against primary sources and NYT's timelines (Michigan early lead, Ohio 2020 lead change, GA 2024 and OH 2022 call times, Nebraska and Maine patterns, Texas canvass and recount). See the corrections section of `STATE_BY_STATE.md`, which supersedes this report where they differ.
>
> **Checked 2026-10-22:** Iowa's polls closed at 9 p.m. CT (10 p.m. ET) in 2020; SF 413 moved the close to 8 p.m. from 2022. So AP's 2020 first votes at 10:11 p.m. ET came 11 minutes after the close, not 71, and there is no conflict with the "10-15 minutes" summary.


*Synthesis of the research notes in `research_notes/Vote count timing in close 2026 races/` (all researched 2026-10-21). Covers the ten races in nine states where the forecast favorite is at 75% or less (see `competitive_races.md`): AK-Sen, GA-Gov, OH-Gov, OH-Sen (special), KS-Sen, TX-Sen, NE-Sen, ME-Sen, IA-Sen and MI-Sen. All clock times are Eastern (ET) on Nov 3-4, 2026 unless marked otherwise. DST ends Nov 1, so ET = EST.*

Seven of the nine states should produce a clear picture on election night. Two will not settle for one to two weeks. **Alaska and Maine are the slow outliers by law.** In Alaska only Election Day and early votes are counted on election night. Absentees are counted Nov 10, 13 and 18. Any ranked-choice tabulation runs on Nov 18, and recent close races were called only on RCV day. In Maine a Collins-Jackson race that falls short of 50% of all ballots, including blanks and the six declared write-ins, goes to a central RCV count that has taken 8-10 days in every case since 2018. Elsewhere, close-of-polls receipt deadlines (GA, OH, IA, MI, NE, ME) or short grace periods (TX to Nov 4, KS to Nov 6 under an injunction) leave only small post-election pools. In those states a race inside about 1-2 points usually waits until Wednesday, not weeks. **Michigan is the main exception among the fast states.** Its close statewide races (2020 Senate, 2024 Senate, the Aug 2026 Senate primary) were called 14-25 hours after polls closed because Wayne County reports last. **The early lead is a trap in almost every state, but its direction differs.** Georgia, Ohio, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and Nebraska have historically shown a Democratic (or Osborn-leaning) first drop that shrinks as Election Day precincts report. Michigan and Alaska show the reverse: the Republican leads early and the Democrat gains late. Maine's town-by-town reporting may show no strong timing pattern at all. Recount triggers matter for the closest outcomes. They are automatic in Georgia (governor at 0.5% or less, a 2026 law), Ohio (0.25%), Michigan (0.1%), Nebraska (1% of the leader's vote, waivable) and Iowa (state-ordered under 0.15%, on request). They are request-only in Kansas, Alaska and Maine. For Texas the recount rules were not verified.

## How to read this report

Each state section separates three kinds of statement. **Verified** means a cited source in the notes, with primary statutes, official calendars or official media packets where the researchers could reach them. **Inference** means the researchers' own reasoning from those facts. Inferences are often well grounded but have not been checked against outcomes. **Unverified / conflict** marks items the researchers could not confirm, items resting only on search snippets, and places where sources disagree. Where the national notes' nine-state comparison table conflicts with a state's own notes, this report follows the state notes, because those researchers read the primary statutes. The conflicts are listed in their own section. Facts in the notes without a source link have been left out.

## Where the national comparison table is wrong, the state notes win

The national notes say every cell marked "(U)" in their nine-state table came from model background knowledge and needed state verification ([NCSL Table 16](https://ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-16-when-absentee-mail-ballot-processing-and-counting-can-begin) was their source for pre-processing). Most of those cells held up. Poll-close times in Georgia, Ohio, Kansas, Nebraska, Maine, Iowa and Michigan were confirmed. Georgia's county (~day 6) and state (~day 17) certification and Ohio's 0.25% recount trigger also check out. Six items do not hold up, and this report uses the state version in each case.

Two national inferences also clash with the state notes. The national notes put Michigan among the "fastest election-night counts" and group Michigan, Maine and Nebraska with states that show a Democratic-leaning first drop. The Michigan notes document a Republican-early, Democratic-late pattern, with close races called the next day. The Maine notes infer that towns report combined absentee and in-person totals, so Maine should show no strong early-blue pattern. The Nebraska notes found no evidence that the 2024 first dump was Osborn-led statewide. Several national cells were filled in by the state notes. Nebraska's canvass is set at Nov 30 and its recount at Dec 2. Maine certifies within 20 days (§722). Alaska targets Nov 25. Iowa's state canvass is due Nov 30. Texas's state canvass can start no earlier than Nov 21, and the Texas notes did not research recount rules, so the national "no automatic recount; petition" cell for Texas remains unverified.

## Federal fights changed little for Nov 3

**Verified.** The Supreme Court decided *Watson v. RNC* on June 29, 2026, 5-4. It held that federal Election Day statutes "do not set a deadline for ballot receipt," so existing state grace periods stand ([Wikipedia: Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_v._Republican_National_Committee); [NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)). Among the nine states, only Alaska (10 days), Texas (5 p.m. the next day) and Kansas (3 days, via injunction) accept late domestic mail. Ohio ended its grace period for domestic voters in 2025 before Watson came down. Executive Order 14399 (March 31, 2026) would have barred USPS from carrying ballots for voters missing from a federal citizenship list. On Sept 14 the Supreme Court left a lower-court freeze of the USPS plan in place, and Votebeat concluded that mail voting "will largely work as it has in past elections" ([Votebeat](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/09/15/trump-mail-voting-executive-order-supreme-court-decision-2026-election/)). The SAVE Act had stalled in the Senate as of late July ([Daily Signal](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/07/30/where-each-senate-republican-stands-on-the-save-act/)). Since Dec 24, 2025, USPS has said a postmark "does not necessarily indicate the first day" it held a piece of mail. That matters only in the three postmark-grace states ([Stateline](https://stateline.org/2025/12/30/usps-says-mail-in-ballots-might-not-get-postmark-on-same-day-theyre-dropped-off/)). AP does not call a race before a state's last polls close, and it declares a winner only when trailing candidates have no path ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/how-the-ap-calls-races-and-what-to-expect-on-election-night)).

**Inference and caution.** The national researcher found no developments between Sept 15 and Oct 21. ABC's report that parts of the order could proceed probably describes the earlier Aug 24 ruling ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/usps-makes-new-rules-mail-ballots/story?id=135879673)). The researcher also did not confirm the name of the Kansas injunction case or whether it is under appeal; the Kansas notes fill that gap. The bigger 2026 effect is behavioral. Democratic confidence in on-time USPS delivery fell from 86% to 60%, and Georgia mail requests fell 52% in strong-Harris precincts ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/early-vote-analysis-mail-ballot-concerns-are-changing-democrats-vote-rcna602489)). The researcher inferred that this could shrink the familiar mail-ballot "mirages." No source quantified the share of the vote counted by midnight in 2022 or 2024 for any of the nine states.

## State by state

### Alaska Senate (Sullivan R vs. Peltola D): RCV day, Nov 18, is the likely decision date

**Rules (verified).** RCV applies on Nov 3. Ballot Measure 2, the repeal, is on the same ballot and cannot change this count ([Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-05/what-to-know-about-alaskas-general-election)). Polls are open 7 a.m.-8 p.m. local time. DOE expects first unofficial results between 8:45 and 9 p.m. AKST, and it says when it is done for the night. Election night includes Election Day ballots and early votes cast Oct 19-29. Early votes cast from the Friday before through Election Day, plus absentee and questioned ballots, are counted on Nov 10 (day 7), Nov 13 (day 10) and Nov 18 (day 15). DOE "may" hold more counts between days 7 and 10 ([DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf); [DOE Counting Schedule](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/ballot-counting-process/counting-system-and-schedule/)). Domestic mail must be postmarked by Election Day and received by Nov 13; mail from abroad has until Nov 18 ([AS 15.20.081](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.081)). Alaska has no cure process and no signature verification. SB 64 would have added both, but it was vetoed and the override failed 38-22 on May 4 ([Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/alaska-legislature/2026-05-04/veto-override-vote-on-elections-bill-fails-as-two-southeast-alaska-lawmakers-flip)). Election-night reports show first choices only. The RCV Detailed Report comes "once tabulation is completed on November 18" ([DOE Media Packet](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/mp/2026/MediaPacket-2026-General.%20V3.pdf)). Certification is targeted for Nov 25, and recount requests are due Nov 30. The state pays for a recount if the margin is 20 votes or fewer or under 0.5%; recounts are automatic only in a tie ([AS 15.20.450](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#15.20.450); [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/2024/11/16/election-update-begich-declares-victory-margin-for-ranked-choice-repeal-now-under-900-votes/)).

**History (verified).** Researcher computations from DOE precinct files ([DOE results](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election-results/)) show absentees at about 22.7% of 2024 ballots, and they leaned clearly more Democratic. Begich's two-party share was 53.5% on Election Day but 42.6% among absentees. In 2022, Tshibaka won Election Day precincts by 9,265 votes and Murkowski won absentees by 8,396. Tshibaka led on election night, 44.36% to 42.69%, and Murkowski won first choices by 2,015 votes. Both 2022 federal races were decided by the RCV tabulation on Nov 23 ([Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/2022/11/23/murkowski-wins-alaskas-u-s-senate-race/)). In 2024, DDHQ projected Begich on Nov 16 and the RCV count on Nov 20 made it final ([Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/2024/11/16/election-update-begich-declares-victory-margin-for-ranked-choice-repeal-now-under-900-votes/); [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/top-stories/2024-11-20/the-results-are-in-nick-begich-iii-has-won-alaskas-u-s-house-race)). The 2024 repeal measure led on election night and lost by about 700 votes. In 2020, under plurality rules, Sullivan's lead fell from 57,866 before absentees to about 44,000 after, and AP called the race Nov 11 ([WTNH/AP](https://www.wtnh.com/news/national/republican-dan-sullivan-re-elected-in-alaska-senate-race/)).

**Inference.** Sullivan should lead the election-night report even in a race that ends tied. Using the 2024 pattern, the researcher estimates Peltola can trail by about 2-4 points on election night and still be on track. The path is not monotonic. Begich's lead grew from election night until Nov 12 as late precincts and late early votes arrived, then fell as absentees were counted. Questioned ballots leaned R in 2024. If no one clears 50% of first choices, expect no formal result before Nov 18. A media call could come earlier only if the first-choice math is decisive, as DDHQ's was in 2024.

**Unverified / conflict.** The Aleutian close time and the list of affected precincts were not confirmed. DOE's media packet lists only Election Day and early ballots for election night, while the statute allows counting reviewed absentees from 8 p.m. In practice the share of absentees counted that night has been small. The 2024 Measure 2 margin is -737 in the DOE file and -664 as reported unofficially. The 2022 RCV totals differ slightly between Wikipedia and Ballotpedia. AP call dates for 2022 and 2024 were not confirmed. The full 2026 Senate top-four list was not found, so the researchers could not say how third- and fourth-candidate transfers would fall.

### Georgia Governor (Bottoms D vs. Jackson R): about 70% at 8 p.m., then a red shift, with a hand recount if the margin is 0.5% or less

**Rules (verified).** Polls close at 7 p.m. Under SB 189 (2024), every absentee ballot accepted by the Monday before, and every advance vote, must be tabulated and reported by 8 p.m. or within one hour of the county's last poll closing, whichever is later ([SB 189 text](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2024-signed-legislation/sb-189/download)). Absentee scanning may begin Oct 12. Fulton County says it starts Oct 26 ([Fulton County](https://www.fultoncountyga.gov/Inside-Fulton-County/Fulton-County-Departments/Registration-and-Elections)). Mail ballots are due at 7 p.m. on Election Day, and UOCAVA ballots by Nov 6 ([georgia.gov](https://georgia.gov/node/34081); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-21-elections/ga-code-sect-21-2-386/)). Counties certify by Nov 9 and the state by Nov 20 ([Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/georgia-election-certification-processes-and-guardrails)). The runoff date is Dec 1 ([georgia.gov](https://georgia.gov/node/34081)). A June 2026 law makes a full hand recount automatic for Governor or Lt. Governor when the margin is 0.5% or less. The SoS orders it within two business days after county certification ([Cobb Courier](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/10/which-comes-first-after-election-day-certification-or-a-recount/)). In 2024 the SoS predicted that 70% of votes would be known by 8 p.m. ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2024/10/23/raffensperger-39-million-georgians-expected-cast-ballots-before-early-voting-ends)). By Oct 21, 2026, 1,055,931 ballots had been cast ([Tifton Gazette](https://tiftongazette.com/2026/10/21/georgia-early-voting-tops-1-million-ballots/)).

**History (verified).** Before SB 189, counts dragged on. Urban precincts at 8:30-10 p.m. Rural Election Day precincts at 10-11:30 p.m., moving toward Hinson. The Republican-early-voting push makes a large Democratic early lead less likely than in 2018 or 2020. A race inside about 2 points probably stays uncalled until after midnight, and inside about 0.5% may wait for the Nov 10 canvass. **Rules (verified).** Most polls close at 8 p.m. ET. ## Conclusion

The ten races sort into three timing groups. The first group can resolve on election night when margins exceed roughly 2 points: Georgia, Ohio (both races), Texas, Iowa, Nebraska and Kansas. In these states the main risk is misreading a Democratic- or Osborn-tilted first drop that is built into the reporting order. The second group is Michigan, where the bias runs the other way and a close race resolves Wednesday afternoon rather than in a week. The third group is Alaska and Maine, where the law guarantees a slow count: the early-trailing Democrat in Alaska and any sub-50% leader in Maine wait 8-15 days. For anyone reading live results or prediction markets, the useful number is not the leader at 10 p.m. but where the remaining vote comes from. That means metro Election Day precincts in Georgia and Texas, rural precincts in Iowa, Nebraska and Kansas, Wayne County in Michigan, absentee count days in Alaska, and blank-plus-write-in share in Maine.

Three open items deserve checking before Nov 3. Georgia's Libertarian withdrawal and the exact wording of its 2026 hand-recount law decide whether the governor's race carries any runoff or extended-recount risk. The Kansas injunction must survive to Nov 6 for the grace period to apply. Every state lacks a published 2026 SoS timing statement, and AP's "What to expect" pages, which typically appear in late October, were not yet available on Oct 21. Those pages would also supply the missing midnight-share figures for 2022 and 2024, which no state's notes could source.
