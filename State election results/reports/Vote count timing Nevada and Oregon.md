# Late Mail Keeps Nevada and Oregon Counting

Neither Nevada nor Oregon is likely to settle a close governor's race on election night. Both mail every voter a ballot, both count mail that arrives days after Nov 3, and both leave Election Day drop-offs for Wednesday and later. **Nevada** releases nothing until the last voter in line statewide has voted, so its first numbers land at about midnight to 2 a.m. ET. Roughly 100,000-150,000 mail ballots, almost all in Clark and Washoe counties, are then counted through the following week, under a receipt deadline of Sat Nov 7, a cure deadline of Mon Nov 9 and a canvass on Fri Nov 13. **Oregon** posts a large first batch just after 11 p.m. ET, which has held roughly 60-80% of the final vote in recent cycles. But the quarter of ballots dropped off on Election Day is counted Wednesday to Friday, postmarked mail counts through Tue Nov 10, and signature cures run to Nov 24. **In both states the post-election count has moved toward the Democrat in every close statewide race documented since 2020.** Nevada's 2022 Senate race went from a Laxalt lead of 19,000-23,000 the morning after to a Cortez Masto win by 7,928, and Kotek's 2022 margin grew from about 15,000 on election night to 66,727. That cuts opposite ways for the model favorites. **Lombardo (R, 69.5%)** needs a Wednesday-morning lead of roughly 25,000-30,000 votes to be safe from a Democratic-leaning tail. **Kotek (D, 70.1%)** should see an election-night lead grow, so the fragile position is a narrow Drazan lead at midnight Pacific time. Close races took three to four days to call in Nevada and two to nine in Oregon, and neither state's officials have published a 2026 estimate of how long the count will take. **Texas governor (Abbott R, 74.0%)** runs on the same fast count as the Texas Senate race, so the Texas section of the earlier nine-state report applies. Roughly two-thirds of the vote posts within an hour of the 8 p.m. ET close, the early Democratic-leaning lead fades as Election Day votes arrive, and only a margin under about 2 points pushes a call past election night.

## Two mail states, one shape: a late first drop and a week-long Democratic tail

The two states differ in their clocks, but their counts have the same shape. The table pulls together the rules and recent history from both states' notes. Every date is computed from the statute and checked against an official 2026 calendar: Clark County's and Washoe's for Nevada, the Secretary of State's for Oregon.

| | **Nevada** (Lombardo R vs. Aaron Ford D) | **Oregon** (Kotek D vs. Drazan R) |
|---|---|---|
| Voting ends | 7 p.m. PST = **10 p.m. ET**. Anyone in line still votes, and **no result may be released until all voting statewide is over** ([NRS 293.305](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec305); [NRS 293.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269935)) | Drop sites close 8 p.m. PST = **11 p.m. ET** (most of Malheur County: 8 p.m. MST = 10 p.m. ET). No tally may be made public before 8 p.m. ([ORS 254.470](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html); [ORS 260.705](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors260.html)) |
| First results | After the Secretary of State says voting has ended statewide. Usually 2-4 hours after polls close, so **about midnight to 2 a.m. ET** (2020: 11:41 p.m. ET) | **Just after 11 p.m. ET.** Every county must post at least once before midnight PST (3 a.m. ET) ([OAR 165-007-0550](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-165-007-0550)) |
| What the first batch holds | Early in-person votes (Oct 17-30) and mail processed before Election Day | Ballots received and scanned through about Monday |
| Election Day drop-offs | Not in the election-night count. Clark's pile (about 54,000 in 2024) posts from Wednesday | About **25%** of all ballots (31-32% in Multnomah and Washington counties), counted Wednesday-Friday |
| Postmarked mail must arrive by | **Sat Nov 7, 5 p.m. PST**; without a readable postmark, Fri Nov 6 | **Tue Nov 10**; a missing postmark counts as mailed Nov 3 |
| Signature cure deadline | **Mon Nov 9, 5 p.m. PST** | **Tue Nov 24** |
| Official results | Mail count must be finished by Tue Nov 10. County canvass **Fri Nov 13**. Supreme Court canvass **Tue Nov 24** | County abstracts due **Mon Nov 30**. The House Speaker opens and publishes the governor returns before the Legislature |
| Recount | **No automatic recount found.** The loser must demand one and prepay by **Tue Dec 1** | **Automatic** if the margin is 0.2% or less of the top two's combined vote (about 3,800-4,000 votes) |

Two comparisons with the nine states in the earlier research stand out. First, **post-Election Day mail matters more here than almost anywhere else in the set.** Among the eleven states now in scope, only Alaska (10 days) gives mail more time than Oregon (7 days) and Nevada (4 days). Kansas (3 days, under an injunction) and Texas (to 5 p.m. Nov 4) have short tails, and Georgia, Ohio, Iowa, Michigan, Nebraska and Maine require receipt by poll close ([NCSL Table 11](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots)). Second, **the direction of the late shift is reversed.** In Georgia, Ohio, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and Nebraska the first drop leans Democratic and Election Day precincts pull the count right. In Nevada and Oregon, the votes counted after election night have been the most Democratic part of the count. On the ET clock these are also the last first results of the night. Oregon's arrive at 11 p.m. ET, when most eastern states have already reported the bulk of their vote, and Nevada's land alongside Alaska's (about 12:45-1 a.m. ET).

## Nevada: Clark County's late mail decides whether Lombardo's lead holds

### The 2021 counting rules still govern; only Clark's paper ballots are new

**The race (verified).** The ballot lists **Joseph Lombardo (R, incumbent), Aaron D. Ford (D, attorney general) and Danielle Ford (no party)**, plus the statutory "None of these candidates" line ([Clark County Fall 2026 Newsletter](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:228bc80b-da3b-42da-bc72-25cce75470d1/original/as/cced-newsletter-fall2026.pdf); [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/elections/2026/general/key)). "None of these candidates" votes must be posted in every result, but only votes for named candidates decide the winner ([NRS 293.269](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269)). The researcher found no majority or runoff rule, so the race is decided by plurality. The two Fords appear one after the other; no reporting says whether that will cost Aaron Ford any votes. Noble Predictive Insights (Oct 7-13) had Lombardo 40, Ford 37 and 23% undecided ([KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/new-poll-shows-lombardo-has-slight-lead-in-2026-governor-matchup), snippet). The market priced Ford as the favorite (58% on Oct 21, per `competitive_races.md`) while the model favored Lombardo. Nevada has no Senate race this year, but the lieutenant governor, attorney general and secretary of state races, NV-1 and NV-3 all come out of the same county batches ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/elections/2026/general/key)).

**Rules for 2026 (verified).** Every active voter registered by Oct 20 is mailed a ballot ([Clark "Facts and Figures"](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:912d393b-be95-46a9-81f0-76326555ef45/original/as/facts-26g.pdf)). A mail ballot counts if it is dropped off by poll close (every polling place has a drop box), or if it is postmarked by Election Day and **received by 5 p.m. on the fourth day after, Sat Nov 7 (8 p.m. ET)**. A ballot whose postmark date cannot be read counts if it arrives by 5 p.m. Fri Nov 6 ([NRS 293.269921](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921); [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)). Voters with a signature problem have until **5 p.m. on the sixth day, Mon Nov 9 (8 p.m. ET)**, to cure it ([NRS 293.269927](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269927); [NV SOS cure page](https://www.nvsos.gov/elections/election-information/2026-election-information/signature-cure), snippet). Provisional ballots are kept out of election-night results, their envelopes may not be opened before 8 a.m. Wednesday, and voters must supply ID by 5 p.m. Fri Nov 6 ([NRS 293.3085](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3085)). Everyone who registers on or after Oct 21, including same-day registrants, votes a provisional ballot ([Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)). Counting boards may begin counting mail ballots on **Mon Oct 19** and must finish by **Tue Nov 10** ([NRS 293.269931](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269931)). Washoe began processing at its central counting place on Oct 9 ([Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php)).

The release rules are what make Nevada's election night late. Disclosing any mail-ballot count before all voting has ended statewide is a misdemeanor, early-voting returns may not be reported until the polls have closed, and leaking them is a gross misdemeanor ([NRS 293.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269935); [NRS 293.3606](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3606)). Because voters already in line at 7 p.m. PST must be allowed to vote ([NRS 293.305](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec305)), Clark says its results are "available after 7pm on Election Day after the Nevada Secretary of State has indicated all statewide voting has finished" ([Clark 2026 election page](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/26g-info)). By law, mail and early-vote returns are reported separately from other votes ([NRS 293.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269935); [NRS 293.3606](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec3606)).

The official calendar runs as follows. Counties must canvass by **Fri Nov 13** ([NRS 293.387](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec387)). The Supreme Court justices and the Secretary of State canvass statewide races on the fourth Tuesday, **Nov 24** ([NRS 293.395](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec395)). A defeated governor candidate must demand a recount, and prepay its estimated cost, within three working days of that canvass, which Clark puts at **5 p.m. Tue Dec 1** after the Thanksgiving holidays. A recount must start within 5 days of the demand and finish within 5 days of starting ([NRS 293.403](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec403); [NRS 293.405](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec405); [Clark 2026 dates](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:a5950eb8-cb6d-404e-a533-b4856da8aa86/original/as/2026-dates.pdf)). The researcher found no automatic-recount provision. Governor contests are excluded from the district-court contest procedure ([NRS 293.407](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec407)); the forum that would hear one was not verified.

**What changed since 2022 (verified).** For counting, almost nothing. AB 321 (2021) created the 4-day receipt window, the 3-day no-postmark rule, the 6-day cure, the counting window and the release ban ([NRS 293.269921-.269935](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec269921)), so **2022 and 2024 ran under the same clock as 2026. 2020 did not**: that year mail postmarked by Election Day counted through Nov 10, seven days later ([AP explainer via News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/explainer-why-ap-called-nevada-and-the-whole-presidential-race-for-biden)). In 2025 Lombardo vetoed the voter-ID bill (AB 499) and the drop-box, omnibus and primary bills (AB 306, AB 534, AB 597). "Republican efforts to impose stricter mail ballot deadlines and signature-curing rules did not become law," and the Secretary of State's push for faster counting also failed ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/how-different-will-nevadas-elections-look-in-2026); [Ballotpedia News](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/06/16/nevada-gov-lombardo-vetoes-bills-to-require-voter-id-and-open-primaries-to-unaffiliated-voters/)). On Oct 28, 2024 the Nevada Supreme Court unanimously upheld counting ballots without a readable postmark that arrive within three days. Republicans had identified only 24 such ballots in the June 2024 primary ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/nevada-supreme-court-rules-non-postmarked-ballots-can-be-counted-within-3-days-of-election); [KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/political/elections-local/nevada-supreme-court-rejects-rncs-argument-that-certain-mail-in-ballots-shouldnt-be-counted)). The one practical change is Clark's in-person process: voters mark on a device, print and review a paper ballot, then scan it ([Clark Fall 2026 Newsletter](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:228bc80b-da3b-42da-bc72-25cce75470d1/original/as/cced-newsletter-fall2026.pdf); [Clark "New Voting Process"](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/services/new-voting-process)). Officials say it should cut adjudication of unclear marks, the main past bottleneck ([KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/big-change-coming-for-nevada-voting-in-2026-election-cycle), snippet). It went statewide only in the June 2026 primary ([Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/jun/09/clark-county-voters-will-see-new-paper-ballot-syst/), snippet), so it has not been tested in a high-turnout general. A proposal to require Clark and Washoe to log mail ballots within 12 hours and review signatures within 24 hours is aimed at a future session, not 2026 ([Nevada Current](https://nevadacurrent.com/2026/08/03/lawmakers-eye-proposals-to-shield-elections-from-federal-interference-speed-up-ballot-processing/), snippet).

### Election night starts after midnight ET and ends with a tenth or more of the vote still out

**How the count is reported (verified).** Clark cast **69% of the 2020 statewide vote** ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-nevada-on-election-day)). As of Oct 20, Clark accounted for 66% of 2026 early ballots, Washoe 21% and the rural counties 13% ([Nevada Independent early-voting blog](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-early-voting-blog-2026-general)). Clark's 2024 plan made the first post, released "once the final vote has been cast," the early-voting results plus mail received through Monday. Its second update added Election Day in-person results ([News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/election-timeline-clark-county-nevada-las-vegas-voting-ballot-presidential-race-white-house-kamala-harris-donald-trump-registrar-canvas-governor)). Washoe says its first 2026 release will hold early votes and mail processed by that point, updated through the night as vote-center ballots arrive ([Washoe timeline](https://www.washoecounty.gov/voters/information/timeline.php); [This Is Reno](https://thisisreno.com/2026/10/washoe-voting-mail-ballot-delays/), snippet). In 2020 the first Nevada votes appeared at **11:41 p.m. ET**, and about **79%** of the total was in by 6 a.m. ET ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-nevada-on-election-day)). Lines can push the first release later. In what the researcher infers was 2024, waits in Nye and Washoe ran about three hours, Washoe's last voter cast a ballot at about 9:45 p.m. PST (12:45 a.m. ET), and Clark posted an overnight update at 1:32 a.m. PST (4:32 a.m. ET) ([Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/news/politics-and-government/nevada/long-lines-in-nye-county-could-delay-results-release-3206876/), snippet; [Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/news/politics-and-government/clark-county/clark-county-to-give-ballot-counting-updates-3207606/), snippet). The Nevada Independent's 2026 guide says the first batch "usually takes between two and four hours after polls close" and that tight races "may be deemed too close to call for several days" ([Nevada Independent FAQ](https://thenevadaindependent.com/elections/2026/faq)).

**Ballots left after election night (verified).** The decisive ballots are the mail dropped off on Election Day, which Nevada does not count that night. In 2022, about 18 hours after polls closed, more than 60,000 Washoe mail ballots and at least 84,000 in Clark were still uncounted, including about 56,900 dropped in Clark's Election Day boxes ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/clark-and-washoe-counties-still-processing-tens-of-thousands-of-mail-ballots-that-could-swing-election-leads), snippet; [KUNR](https://www.kunr.org/politics-and-policy/2022-11-09/2022-election-more-than-100-000-ballots-pending-count-washoe-clark-county-nevada)). On election night a Secretary of State spokeswoman said, "Clark is not counting drop boxes tonight" ([Nevada Independent live blog](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/2022-election-live-blog-will-rain-snow-affect-election-day-turnout), snippet). Even on the Saturday that decided the Senate race, up to 12,000 Clark ballots were still due the following week, including more than 7,100 needing cures and 5,500 provisionals ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/cortez-masto-defeats-laxalt-in-senate-race-securing-majority-for-democrats)). In 2024, Clark registrar Lorena Portillo said on Wednesday afternoon that about **55,000 mail ballots** still had to be posted and about **12,000 needed cures**, while Washoe had about **45,000** to count ([News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/jacky-rosen-takes-lead-sam-brown-nevada-us-senate-race-ballot-counting-clark-washoe-county-las-vegas-reno-election-voting-congress-politics)). Secretary of State Aguilar has said that about 99% of Clark's 2024 ballots were in hand by the end of Election Day, yet only about 90% of results were announced that night ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/how-different-will-nevadas-elections-look-in-2026)). Reconciliation can still find ballots: before its 2024 canvass Clark found 1,608 timely mail ballots that had never been tabulated, and no outcome changed ([Fox5](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2024/11/15/1608-mail-ballots-found-by-clark-county-ahead-certification/)).

**After election night (verified).** Washoe will post "by noon and 5 p.m. each day while significant numbers of ballots remain" (**3 p.m. and 8 p.m. ET**). It adopted that schedule in the June 2026 primary, replacing once-a-day posting ([This Is Reno](https://thisisreno.com/2026/10/washoe-voting-mail-ballot-delays/), snippet). Clark has published no 2026 post-election schedule ([Clark 2026 election page](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/elections/26g-info)). In 2024 it posted Wednesday afternoon and evening, then "by 8 p.m." PST (11 p.m. ET) Thursday to Saturday, warning that the times could change ([News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/election-timeline-clark-county-nevada-las-vegas-voting-ballot-presidential-race-white-house-kamala-harris-donald-trump-registrar-canvas-governor)). Its decisive Saturday drop in 2022 came at about 6:15 p.m. PST, or 9:15 p.m. ET ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/clark-county-nevada-says-mail-in-ballots-being-counted-results-expected-saturday-evening), snippet). After the June 2026 primary, the Secretary of State issued county-by-county releases on outstanding ballots ([NV SOS release 3758](https://www.nvsos.gov/Home/Components/News/News/3758); [release 3760](https://www.nvsos.gov/Home/Components/News/News/3760), snippets). If the office repeats them in November, they will be the best real-time measure of the vote left (inference).

The 2022 Senate count shows how the tail works. AP's explainer gives the day-by-day path. On Wednesday, Clark had about 600,000 ballots counted (Cortez Masto 52-46) and Washoe about 130,000 (Laxalt more than half), and Laxalt led by more than 19,000; the Nevada Independent put his morning lead as high as 23,000. On Thursday, Clark added about 12,000 votes at 62-35 Cortez Masto and Washoe nearly 40,000 at 61-36, cutting the lead to about 8,000. On Friday, Clark's 27,000 went 63-33 and Washoe's 11,000 went 54-43, leaving Laxalt just over 800 ahead. On Saturday, Clark's roughly 23,000 at 61-36 put Cortez Masto about 5,000 up, and Washoe's 10,000-plus at 56-40 made it 6,556 ([AP via KTNV](https://www.ktnv.com/news/why-ap-called-the-nevada-senate-race-for-catherine-cortez-masto); [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/cortez-masto-defeats-laxalt-in-senate-race-securing-majority-for-democrats)). In 2024, Rosen led by 13,296 on Thursday afternoon (calculated from the reported totals) and by 18,289 by AP's count that evening, and she "consistently won every update" Clark and Washoe released after Election Day ([News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/jacky-rosen-takes-lead-sam-brown-nevada-us-senate-race-ballot-counting-clark-washoe-county-las-vegas-reno-election-voting-congress-politics); [AP via KOLO](https://www.kolotv.com/2024/11/09/why-ap-called-nevada-senate-race-jacky-rosen/)). **By my calculation, the count after Wednesday morning moved the Senate margin about 27,000-31,000 votes toward the Democrat in 2022 (roughly 2.6-3.0 points) and about 24,000-25,000 votes in 2024 (roughly 1.7 points).** Lombardo's own 2022 lead shrank by about 5,000 votes after Friday as later mail, cures and provisionals came in. That is the researcher's reading; no day-by-day governor figures were found.

**Why the tail leans Democratic (verified).** By party registration, which is not the same as votes cast, in-person early voting leans Republican, mail leans Democratic, and Election Day has swung between the two. In 2022, in-person early voting was 28% of turnout and Republicans won it by about 50,000 ballots (49-31). Mail was 51% and Democrats won it by about 70,000 (14 points). Election Day was about a fifth of the vote and went Republican roughly 2-to-1. In 2024, Republicans won in-person early voting by about 97,000 ballots (18 points). Democrats won mail by about 66,000 (9 points), and Election Day was essentially tied. Jon Ralston had expected a late mail surge to help Democrats catch up in 2024 and later wrote that it did not ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-early-voting-blog-2026-general)). AP's summary is that "votes counted after Election Day have been heavily Democratic," with Democrats usually carrying only Clark and Washoe ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/what-to-expect-in-nevada-on-election-day)). The 2026 early vote so far looks similar. As of the evening of Oct 21, registered Democrats led by 10,168 of 244,767 ballots (4 points), about 12% of active voters. Mail was 63% of ballots, with Democrats up 9 points on mail and Republicans up about 3 on in-person ballots, and the regional splits were Clark D+11, Washoe D+7 and the rural counties R+31. Ralston expects 80-85% of the vote to be cast before Nov 3 ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-early-voting-blog-2026-general)).

**What this means for 2026 (inference).** The first release mixes Republican-leaning in-person early votes with Democratic-leaning mail, so it could be close. The Election Day in-person count overnight should lean toward Lombardo. The Election Day drop-offs and late-arriving mail counted Wednesday to Saturday should lean toward Ford, probably somewhere between the 2022 and 2024 strength. Rural ballots still out late in the week favor Lombardo, but AP judged them too few to close Clark and Washoe gaps in both 2022 and 2024. The researcher concluded that a Lombardo lead "in the low five figures" at the end of election night is not safe under a 2022-style tail, and that about 15,000 would probably hold under a 2024-style tail. The 2024 Senate numbers argue for a higher bar: that tail moved about 24,000-25,000 votes even though, on the researcher's reading, it leaned Democratic less heavily than in 2022. On both cycles' evidence, **a Lombardo lead under roughly 25,000-30,000 votes (about 2-2.5 points) on Wednesday morning should be read as at risk**, and a larger one should hold. A race inside about 1 point may not be clear until the Mon Nov 9 cure deadline or the Nov 13 canvass. This year's electorate is smaller than 2024's (1.1-1.2 million projected against roughly 1.46-1.48 million), which shrinks the tail in votes but not necessarily as a share of the vote.

**What officials have said about 2026 (verified).** They have given process and deadlines, not forecasts. The Secretary of State says unofficial results will post on Results.NV.gov after polls close and remain unofficial until the canvass ([NV SOS](https://www.nvsos.gov/elections/election-information/2026-election-information), snippet). Washoe's registrar warned that last-minute mail and postal-service changes could delay results; in the June primary, about 13,000 mail ballots, 20% of all mail Washoe received, arrived on election night ([This Is Reno](https://thisisreno.com/2026/10/washoe-voting-mail-ballot-delays/), snippet). No official has estimated how many days the count will take or how many ballots to expect. AP's general-election "What to expect" for Nevada had not been found as of Oct 22; only the June primary edition was located, and it returned a 404 ([WTOP](https://wtop.com/government/2026/06/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-nevadas-state-primary/)).

## Oregon: an 11 p.m. ET first drop, then three days of Portland-area ballots

### Ballots count if postmarked by Nov 3 and in hand by Nov 10

**The race (verified).** This is a rematch of 2022: Gov. **Tina Kotek** (D, also on the Working Families line) against **Christine Drazan** (R, also the Libertarian nominee), with Pacific Green nominee Brett Smith the only other named candidate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Oregon_gubernatorial_election)). Kotek won in 2022 by **66,727 votes (3.42 points)** while unaffiliated Betsy Johnson took 8.62% ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Oregon_gubernatorial_election)). Smith polls at 3-6%, so this year is closer to a two-way race. Polls released Oct 8 had Kotek ahead 46-44 (Oregonian/DHM, Sept 24-Oct 1) and 46-42 (GBAO, a Kotek internal), after three earlier polls showed Drazan ahead by 4, 2 and 1 points ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/08/new-polls-kotek-may-lead/)). Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections rate the race Lean D; RealClearPolitics calls it a toss-up ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Oregon_gubernatorial_election)). Merkley's Senate seat is not competitive, and there are no statewide ballot measures ([Oregon SOS](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/pages/current-election.aspx)).

**Rules for 2026 (verified).** Ballots went out starting Oct 14 ([SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf)). Drop sites stay open until at least 8 p.m. PST on Election Day. A mailed ballot counts if it arrives by then, or if it carries a postal indicator dated no later than Election Day and **arrives within seven calendar days, by Tue Nov 10**. A ballot with no postmark or an illegible one is treated as mailed on Election Day and counts if it arrives by Nov 10 ([ORS 254.470](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html); [SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf)). Under the implementing rule, late arrivals must come through USPS, the earlier of two postmarks is the official one, officials may use USPS tools or a signed declaration to date an envelope with no postmark, and a late envelope with no signature is not counted ([OAR 165-007-0045](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewSingleRule.action?ruleVrsnRsn=285827)). AP's primary preview says mailed ballots must be "postmarked by 8 p.m. local time" ([AP](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/18/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-oregons-primaries/)), but the statute requires only the date. Ballots dropped off in the wrong county must be forwarded by Nov 12 ([SOS 2026 calendar](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/current-elections-calendar.pdf)).

Counties may open envelopes and scan ballots into the tally system **as soon as they arrive**, under a security plan the Secretary of State has approved ([ORS 254.478 and 254.074](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)). No one may make any tally public before 8 p.m. on Election Day ([ORS 260.705](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors260.html)). The reporting rule requires each county to publish to the state results site at least once between 8 p.m. and midnight PST (11 p.m.-3 a.m. ET), to post results within an hour of releasing them, and to publish its schedule at least 30 days ahead. After Election Day counties report "as frequently as possible." **No count of ballots remaining is required** ([OAR 165-007-0550](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-165-007-0550)). Voters whose signatures are challenged have until the 21st day, **Tue Nov 24**, to cure ([ORS 254.431](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)). Counties must deliver abstracts by the 27th day, **Mon Nov 30**. Multnomah, Washington and Clackamas all plan to certify that day ([ORS 254.545](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html); [Multnomah](https://multco.us/node/654374); [Washington County](http://www.washingtoncountyor.gov/elections/current-election-results); [Clackamas](https://www.clackamas.us/elections/november-3-2026-general-election-unofficial-results)). The Secretary of State canvasses every office except governor by Thu Dec 10 ([ORS 254.555](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors254.html)). The governor's returns are sealed, sent to the Secretary of State addressed to the House Speaker, and opened and published by the Speaker before both houses of the Legislature, which also decides ties and contests ([Oregon Constitution, Art. V §§4-6](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/Pages/OrConst.aspx)).

**Oregon is the one state in this pair with an automatic recount.** The Secretary of State must order a full recount if the gap between the top two is no more than one-fifth of 1% of their combined vote ([ORS 258.280](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors258.html)). The researcher puts that at **about 3,800-4,000 votes** this year; it was 3,535 in 2022. Recounts have no deadline beyond "as soon as practicable," and requested recounts must be demanded by Tue Dec 15 ([ORS 258.221 and 258.161](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors258.html)). AP may still call a race that is subject to a recount if the lead is too large to change ([AP](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/18/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-oregons-primaries/)). For bettors, the practical resolution points are the AP call and the Nov 30 county abstracts, not a state certification (inference).

**What changed since 2022 (verified).** Oregon's counting clock has not changed since 2022. HB 3291 (2021) created the postmark grace period, first used in the 2022 general, lengthened the cure period and raised the county certification window from 20 to 27 days ([KDRV/AP](https://www.kdrv.com/news/your-voice-your-vote-ap-explains-kotek-victory-declaration-while-drazan-waits-for-totals/article_a689e4f8-61ec-11ed-8388-8fae2b3fab46.html); [OAR 165-007-0045](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewSingleRule.action?ruleVrsnRsn=285827); [Multnomah County](https://multco.us/news/news-release-multnomah-county-voters-cast-tens-thousands-ballots-amid-surge-returns-election)). The 2025 bills that would have narrowed "postal indicator" to a USPS cancellation (SB 964) or ended vote-by-mail (SB 210) did not pass ([LPRO session brief](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/lpro/Publications/2025%20-%20General_Government_and_Elections_Session_Brief.pdf)), and the 2026 short session passed nothing on counting ([Ballotpedia News](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/04/23/oregon-enacts-campaign-finance-changes-three-other-election-bills-in-2026/)). **The change that matters is at the post office.** USPS finalized a rule in December 2025 that postmarks reflect the day mail is handled at a processing plant, and in 2025 mail from offices more than 50 miles from a distribution center began moving once a day instead of twice ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/ballots-discounted-for-late-postmarks-surged-in-washington-and-oregon-after-postal-service-change)). In the May 2026 primary, Oregon rejected **7,314 grace-period ballots for late postmarks, 13.1%** of those arriving late, up from 9.5% in 2024 and 8.2% in 2022 ([Oregon SOS](https://apps.oregon.gov/oregon-newsroom/OR/SOS/Posts/Post/oregon-leaders-stand-united-protecting-Vote-By-Mail-elections)). Secretary of State Tobias Read is urging voters to mail by Oct 27 or use a drop box. A $1.25 million emergency allocation funded statewide ballot tracking and more than 20 new drop boxes, bringing the total above 300. An August test found all 108 mail pieces postmarked on time, but one Harney County piece took 11 days to arrive ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/14/oregon-elections-chief-urges-early-voting-usps-service-slow-mail/)).

It reached 44,234 on Thursday morning (calculated from [KPTV](https://www.kptv.com/2022/11/10/live-tina-kotek-campaign-holds-press-conference-after-tweeting-declaration-victory/)) and **64,259 (3.72 points) by Friday midday** ([KDRV/AP](https://www.kdrv.com/news/your-voice-your-vote-ap-explains-kotek-victory-declaration-while-drazan-waits-for-totals/article_a689e4f8-61ec-11ed-8388-8fae2b3fab46.html)), then slipped to the final 3.42. Wednesday's outstanding ballots were "disproportionately" in Multnomah, breaking about 70-20 for Kotek, and in Washington, about 55-36 ([Willamette Week](https://www.wweek.com/news/2022/11/09/tina-kotek-on-track-to-be-oregons-next-governor/)). Kotek claimed victory just after 8 p.m. Wednesday on the Oregonian and OPB projections ([KOIN](https://digital-release.koin.com/news/elections/christine-drazan-yet-to-concede-oregon-governor-race-as-vote-count-continues/)). AP waited until Thursday evening, noting "no signs" that Drazan was gaining ([KDRV/AP](https://www.kdrv.com/news/your-voice-your-vote-ap-explains-kotek-victory-declaration-while-drazan-waits-for-totals/article_a689e4f8-61ec-11ed-8388-8fae2b3fab46.html)), and Drazan conceded Friday afternoon ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2022/11/11/christine-drazan-concession-oregon-governor-race), snippet). The one counterexample is local. In OR-5 in 2022, a Saturday-night drop of about 23,000 Clackamas ballots widened the Republican's lead to about 6,500 ([Salem Reporter](https://www.salemreporter.com/2022/11/14/lori-chavez-deremer-wins-oregons-5th-congressional-district-seat/)).

**Why later returns lean Democratic (verified).** The Secretary of State's daily return reports record the registered party of each ballot received, not votes. The researcher's breakdown of the 2024 reports shows **the Democratic registration edge widening steadily with lateness**: D+8.5 among the 1.43 million ballots received through Monday morning, D+11.0 for Monday through Election Day evening, D+14.3 for the last Election Day drop-offs and **D+17.7 for post-Election Day mail**, where nonaffiliated voters were 41% ([SOS reports of Nov 4](https://web.archive.org/web/20241105065651/https://sos.oregon.gov/voting/Documents/G24-Daily-Ballot-Returns.pdf), [Nov 5](https://web.archive.org/web/20241106082418/https://sos.oregon.gov/voting/Documents/G24-Daily-Ballot-Returns.pdf) and [final](https://web.archive.org/web/20250508110257/https://sos.oregon.gov/voting/Documents/G24-Daily-Ballot-Returns.pdf), archived). In 2022 the gap rose from D+8.4 for early returns to about D+12 for the final weekend and Election Day, then fell back to D+8.0 for ballots logged after Wednesday morning, which were mostly Clackamas's backlog plus grace-period mail ([SOS G22 reports, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20221110100105/https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/statistics/G22-Daily-Ballot-Returns.pdf)). The driver is geography: Multnomah and Washington take about 31-32% of their ballots on Election Day. In vote terms, the Democrat's statewide margin grew after the first release by about 0.35 points in the 2024 presidential race, about 1.1 points in the 2022 Senate race (from 9 p.m. PST) and about 2.4 points in the 2022 governor's race ([Banks Post](https://www.bankspost.com/election-2024-oregon-goes-to-harris/); [KOIN](https://digital-release.koin.com/news/elections/general-election-us-senate-seat-ron-wyden-jo-rae-perkins-11082022/); [Willamette Week](https://www.wweek.com/news/2022/11/09/tina-kotek-on-track-to-be-oregons-next-governor/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Oregon_gubernatorial_election)). Early 2026 returns say little yet. On Oct 21 only 155,137 ballots (4.98% of 3,117,439 active voters) had arrived, and by the researcher's calculation they were 52.4% registered Democrats and 25.5% Republicans ([SOS ballot-return dashboard](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/Ballot-Return-Dashboard-Nov-3-2026.pdf)). That skew is normal this early.

**What this means for 2026 (inference).** The Wednesday-Friday count, dominated by Multnomah and Washington drop-offs and nonaffiliated voters, is the Democratic-leaning part of the count. **A Kotek lead at the end of election night should usually grow by roughly 1-2.5 points, and a Drazan lead of under about 2 points at midnight PST would be at real risk.** The reverse risk comes late in the week. If Clackamas, Marion or other Republican-leaning counties still hold large Election Day batches on Thursday or Friday, the last part of the count can shave a few tenths off a Democratic lead, as it did between Friday and the final count in 2022. The postmarked tail is too small to matter unless the margin is under about 0.5 points, and the higher postmark rejection rate makes it smaller still. With a 2022-like count, the race would be callable by Wednesday or Thursday. A race inside about 1 point could wait, like OR-5 in 2024, for the Nov 10 postmark deadline and the Nov 12 Multnomah update. Prices may move on the Oregonian's and OPB's projections before AP calls.

**What officials have said about 2026 (verified).** Secretary of State Read's public message is about getting ballots in on time. He has not estimated how long the count will take ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/14/oregon-elections-chief-urges-early-voting-usps-service-slow-mail/); [Oregon SOS](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/pages/current-election.aspx)). The concrete commitments are the county schedules above. AP's Oregon general-election "What to expect" had not appeared as of Oct 22; the 2024 edition came out on Oct 25 ([AP via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/?p=1441384)).

**Unverified / conflict.** Oregon publishes no statewide count of ballots remaining, and the reporting rule does not require one. The 2022 governor numbers at the 8 p.m. first release were not found, so the 2.4-point shift is measured from the "about 15,000" election-night figure. The two 2022 progress figures conflict: about 1.5 million counted by Wednesday noon, but only about 72% counted on Thursday morning. They probably use different denominators. The 2024 post-election logging by county (Lane 0, Clackamas 275) looks inconsistent with county practice. The 2024 presidential call time comes from a search excerpt. The sources do not say which parts of Malheur County keep Pacific time, or whether the 8 p.m. embargo means local time there. Lane's and Marion's 2026 schedules were not found.

## Texas governor follows the Senate race's fast early-vote count

Abbott (R, 74.0%) against Gina Hinojosa (D) is on the same ballot as Talarico against Paxton, and the same 254 counties count it in the same batches. **The Texas section of the earlier nine-state report applies unchanged.** Its essentials, as checked there, are these. Polls close at 7 p.m. local time: 8 p.m. ET in most of the state and 9 p.m. ET in El Paso and Hudspeth counties. Counties of 100,000 or more may start counting early ballots when early voting ends on Oct 30 but may announce nothing before polls close. Mail postmarked by Election Day must arrive by **5 p.m. CT Wed Nov 4**. Military and overseas ballots, mail-ballot cures and provisional-voter ID are due Nov 9, and Nov 16 is the last day to count. The local canvass runs Nov 6-17, and the state canvass can start no earlier than Nov 21 ([Texas SOS Advisory 2026-21](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/laws/advisory2026-21-november-3-election-law-calendar.shtml)). Counties must report within 24 hours of polls closing, and once counting starts it may not stop ([Texas SOS](https://www.sos.texas.gov/about/newsreleases/2022/030122.shtml)). AP makes no statewide call before 9 p.m. ET. Counties release all or nearly all early and absentee results in their first update, before Election Day results. A recount is automatic only on a tie, and a losing candidate may request one if within 10% of the leader's vote ([AP Decision Notes, Mar 2026](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/politics/2026/03/02/ap-decision-notes-what-to-expect-in-the-texas-state-primaries/)). In 2024, 75.8% of Texans voted early in person, 3.3% by mail and 20.3% on Election Day ([EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/2024_EAVS_Data_Brief_TX.pdf)).

**History (verified).** AP called Abbott's 2022 race against O'Rourke on election night, saying Abbott "never trailed" ([AP via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/why-ap-called-texas-governor-041929806.html)). The project's NYT-derived timeline (`STATE_BY_STATE.md`) differs only on the first minutes: O'Rourke led the earliest returns until 8:37 p.m. ET, AP called the race at 10:56 p.m. ET, and Abbott won by 10.9 points. The same timelines show 62-67% of the Texas vote counted one hour after the close in 2020 and 2024. The 2024 Senate race was called at 11:39 p.m. ET with about 76% counted ([AP via Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/politics/2024/11/06/why-ap-called-the-texas-senate-race-for-ted-cruz/)). In 2018, O'Rourke led the early returns against Cruz and the lead traded for about three hours ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2018/11/06/ted-cruz-beto-orourke-texas-midterm-election-results)).

**Inference.** Texas is the opposite of Nevada and Oregon. Its Democratic-leaning moment is the first early-vote dump at about 8 p.m. ET, and Election Day ballots then pull the count toward the Republican. An early Hinojosa lead is therefore no signal, and Abbott's position should improve from about 8:30-9 p.m. ET onward. A margin above about 2 points should be called on election night. Under that, the call slides past midnight CT. The post-election tail of late mail, overseas ballots, cures and provisionals is small enough to matter only within about 0.5 points.

## Federal courts kept both grace periods; the post office is the live risk

**Verified.** In *Watson v. RNC* (June 29, 2026, 5-4), the Supreme Court held that the federal Election Day statutes do not preempt state deadlines for receiving mail ballots cast on time, and it reversed the Fifth Circuit ([Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/609/24-1260/); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/supreme-court-holds-federal-election-day-statutes-do-not-preempt-state-receipt-deadlines-for-timely-cast-mail-ballots/); [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2026/6/supreme-court-decides-watson-v-republican-national-committee-et-al)). Nevada's 4-day and Oregon's 7-day windows therefore stand for House races too; the governor's races were always governed by state law. On Jan 9, 2026, a federal judge permanently blocked enforcement of the March 2025 executive order's ballot-receipt provisions against Oregon and Washington ([Washington State Standard](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2026/01/09/repub/federal-judge-blocks-trump-election-order-siding-with-oregon-washington/)). On Sept 14 the Supreme Court left in place a lower court's freeze of the 2026 USPS ballot plan, so mail voting "will largely work as it has in past elections" ([Votebeat](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/09/15/trump-mail-voting-executive-order-supreme-court-decision-2026-election/)). The remaining federal factor is the post office. Since Dec 24, 2025, USPS has said a postmark "does not necessarily indicate the first day" it held a piece of mail ([Stateline](https://stateline.org/2025/12/30/usps-says-mail-in-ballots-might-not-get-postmark-on-same-day-theyre-dropped-off/)). Any post office will hand-stamp a ballot for free on request ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/ballots-discounted-for-late-postmarks-surged-in-washington-and-oregon-after-postal-service-change)).

**Inference.** In the nine-state research this mattered only in Alaska, Texas and Kansas. It matters more in Nevada and Oregon. Oregon's late-postmark rejection rate has already risen to 13.1% (May 2026), and Washoe's registrar has warned that postal changes could slow the count. A ballot dropped in a collection box on Nov 2 or 3 that is not postmarked until Nov 4 is rejected in both states. Nevada's three-day rule for ballots with no readable postmark does not rescue one whose postmark is legible but late. Both states' officials are steering voters to drop boxes. Voters who switch from mail to drop boxes move their ballots out of the postmarked tail and into the Election Day drop-off pile, which is counted Wednesday to Friday either way. So the late Democratic shift should arrive on roughly the same schedule, with fewer ballots in the final Nov 7-10 trickle. One residual legal question: two Nevada justices wrote separately in the 2024 no-postmark case, and one said the statute "is clear and unambiguous that a mail ballot must contain a postmark" ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/nevada-supreme-court-rules-non-postmarked-ballots-can-be-counted-within-3-days-of-election)). That leaves room for a post-election challenge to such ballots, though their numbers are tiny.

## Conclusion

Nevada and Oregon belong with Alaska, not with the fast states of the earlier report. In all three the Republican's position is strongest at the end of election night and the Democrat gains as mail is counted, the mirror image of the early-vote states where the Democrat's best moment is the first hour. Unlike Alaska, which counts on fixed days and then runs a ranked-choice tabulation, Nevada and Oregon count every day, so their tails resolve in days rather than two weeks. That creates an asymmetry for the two model favorites. **Lombardo's numbers will look their best at the end of election night and Kotek's their worst**, so an election-night snapshot flatters the Republican in both states, and a model-versus-market comparison made on election night should be adjusted for it. On Wednesday morning, the number to watch is the size and location of the uncounted pile, not the leader. In Nevada, that means the Election Day drop-off counts Clark and Washoe announce and any outstanding-ballot release from the Secretary of State, set against a tail that moved close Senate races by roughly 24,000-31,000 votes in 2022 and 2024. In Oregon, it means how much of the uncounted vote sits in Multnomah and Washington rather than Clackamas and Marion. Markets that settle on certification rather than a media call will wait longer: Nevada's statewide canvass is Nov 24, and Oregon's governor result is never certified by the Secretary of State at all. Instead the House Speaker publishes it before the 2027 Legislature, on a date the research did not pin down.

A few items are worth checking before Nov 3. AP's general-election "What to expect" pages for Nevada and Oregon had not appeared by Oct 22; Oregon's 2024 edition came out on Oct 25. Clark County has not posted a post-election release schedule. It is not yet known whether Nevada's Secretary of State will repeat the primary's daily outstanding-ballot releases, which would be the best live gauge of the tail. Clark's new print-and-scan ballots have not been tested at general-election turnout, and Lane and Marion counties have not published schedules. Texas needs nothing beyond what the earlier report already covers.
